The Morning Picture

The Cybercab goes on sale, by invitation

Tesla began inviting selected customers on Friday evening to buy a Cybercab for personal use at an estimated $29,990, with a required $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription and deliveries estimated for December in its Texas Robotaxi cities, EV (eletric-vehicles.com) reported. The two-seater has no steering wheel or pedals and will drive only inside Tesla's Robotaxi service areas. Within hours Elon Musk said the monthly fee was too high and should fall to about $115.

Tesla's charging account announced 26 new Supercharger sites and a Megacharger in the past week, Robotaxi widened its Miami service area, and on Monday the Dutch vehicle authority approved a stricter version of Tesla Assisted Driving for Europe. TSLA fell 1.8% to $390.22 on Friday, before the Cybercab announcement.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Tesla opens Cybercab orders to invited buyers

$29,990 plus $299 a month, Texas only, December deliveries; the Netherlands approves a stricter Assisted Driving update, and Robotaxi grows in Miami.

"Select customers are now being invited to purchase Cybercab for personal use," Tesla's Robotaxi account posted at 6:27 p.m. EST Friday, after the market closed. The order page it showed lists an estimated price of $29,990, a $1,000 non-refundable deposit and a $299 monthly Autonomy Subscription, with the first month included, and estimates delivery in December 2026. Buyers must take delivery in Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio or Fort Worth, the five Texas cities where Robotaxi operates, according to the order page shared by YouTuber Marques Brownlee, EV reported.

"It'll work unsupervised inside all the same geo locations as @robotaxi, just serving the owners' needs," Ashok Elluswamy (@aelluswamy), Tesla's vice president of AI software, posted at 7:35 p.m. EST. Asked whether an owner could keep the car at home inside the service area, he replied: "Yes, it can park in your driveway."

"It will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval. But the monthly fee is too high. Should match the cost of Tesla FSD and connectivity for other models, which is more like ~$115/month."

Elon Musk (@elonmusk), replying to Marques Brownlee, Sat Nov 14, 1:38 a.m. EST

Tesla charges $99 a month for FSD (Supervised) in the United States and $9.99 a month for Premium Connectivity; Musk did not say when the Cybercab fee would change. The current Cybercab has a 47.6 kilowatt-hour (kWh) battery and an estimated 293 miles of range, and charges only at NACS fast chargers, including Superchargers, EV reported.

What the subscription terms say

Screenshots of Tesla's Autonomy Subscription Agreement, posted on Friday by the X user Spencer (@scotsrule08), say "your Cybercab will not operate without an active Autonomy Subscription," TeslaNorth reported. The car is for personal, non-commercial use and may not be put on Tesla's Robotaxi network or on ride-hailing, delivery or rental platforms unless Tesla authorizes it. It runs only inside Tesla-enabled Robotaxi geofences, which Tesla can expand, shrink or move. Touchscreen controls can move it short distances at up to 2 mph, and three or more strikeouts for unsafe use bring a one-week suspension.

The subscription covers tire and windshield replacement, wipers, cabin air filters, inspections and towing of up to about 50 miles; owners pay for insurance, registration, charging and parking. The order agreement bars a sale or transfer without Tesla's written consent, EV reported.

Dutch regulator approves a stricter Assisted Driving update

The Netherlands' vehicle authority, the RDW, said on Monday that it has approved an update to Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD), the European name for FSD (Supervised), for cars with Hardware 4 (HW4) or later. Drivers will get earlier inattention warnings and a "full strike" if they are not paying attention while the system performs a maneuver. Setting a maximum speed above the one the system shows now needs an upward scroll of the wheel, the speed-limit sign pulses red if the car then drives faster, and above 130 km/h drivers must keep their hands on the wheel.

The RDW said its own track and public-road tests found "no adverse or unintended effects." It has informed the 10 EU countries that adopted its approval: Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Croatia, Latvia, Lithuania, Slovenia, Slovakia and the Czech Republic. EU-wide approval is still before the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV), and Tesla decides when to release the update over the air.

Quick Hit

Cybercabs in Dallas. Videos posted on X on Saturday showed dozens of gold Cybercabs parked in rows in a Dallas lot, some delivered by car transporter, EV reported. Elluswamy said on the Oct 21 earnings call that Cybercab would launch in Dallas this month; Tesla has not announced rides there.

Robotaxi widens its Miami area

"Now serving more of Miami," the Robotaxi account posted at 2:44 a.m. EST Monday. Miami is one of Robotaxi's eight cities, with Orlando and Tampa in Florida and five in Texas, though the Cybercab order page lists no Florida delivery locations, EV noted.

JPMorgan sees Robotaxi catching Waymo's miles

Tesla's Robotaxi network could match Waymo's US autonomous mileage in the second half of 2027 with about 18,000 active vehicles, JPMorgan analyst Rajat Gupta wrote on Monday, keeping a Neutral rating and a $430 target, EV reported. The Texas Department of Motor Vehicles listed 1,303 vehicles under Tesla Robotaxi LLC on Saturday, 573 Cybercabs and 730 Model Ys, up from 544 and 507 in late October. The bank put cumulative unsupervised miles at about 5.3 million.

FSD on a Times Square billboard

Tesla is running its first Times Square billboard for FSD through the end of November, TeslaNorth reported, citing Sawyer Merritt. The display reads "7x safer than a human when engaged. 15+ billion miles driven globally."

II · Sales & Demand

The cheapest Model Y now goes 348 miles

The Rear-Wheel Drive gains 27 miles at the same $39,990; only the $109,990 Cyberbeast is left to order, and Canadian Model Y waits run to May.

Tesla's US configurator now rates the Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive at 348 miles of estimated range, up 27 miles or about 8.5% from 321, with the price unchanged at $39,990, Drive Tesla reported on Sunday. Tesla has not said what changed, and the Environmental Protection Agency's fueleconomy.gov still listed 321 miles on Sunday. With the $41,990 All-Wheel Drive gone from the order page, the Rear-Wheel Drive is the only entry-level Model Y; all-wheel drive starts at $49,990 with the Premium.

The Cyberbeast is the only Cybertruck left to order

The tri-motor Cyberbeast, now $109,990 after a $7,500 increase, is the only Cybertruck that can be selected on Tesla's US configurator; the Dual Motor and Premium All-Wheel Drive are still listed but greyed out, Electrek reported on Friday. The Cyberbeast cost $102,490 a week earlier. Musk extended Cybertruck production on Nov 11 through the end of summer 2027 to work through more of the order backlog.

Canadian Model Y waits reach six months

New Model Y orders in Canada now show estimated delivery between March and May 2027 for all three trims, two months later than earlier in the week, EV reported on Friday after checking postal codes in eight provinces. Prices are unchanged, with the Rear-Wheel Drive at C$49,990 before the federal rebate of up to C$5,000.

Quick Hit

Canada's luxury tax. Ottawa proposes lifting the luxury tax threshold for fully electric and hydrogen vehicles from C$100,000 to C$150,000, effective right away pending Parliament's approval, Drive Tesla reported.

III · Energy & Charging

A new fleet takes its first Semi

EV Semi Fleet's truck arrives in San Bernardino; the week's new Supercharger and Megacharger sites follow in Section IV.

EV Semi Fleet, a California freight operator, has taken delivery of its first production Tesla Semi in San Bernardino, TeslaNorth reported on Sunday. Founder and CEO Jacob Guerra said its goal is to help shippers move freight with zero tailpipe emissions. Sawyer Merritt reported that the truck is a Long Range Semi and that the fleet expects seven trucks in the near future, with plans for the Port of Long Beach and Florida and Georgia routes in the third quarter of 2027.

Tesla rates the top Semi at 500 miles at a full 82,000-pound gross combination weight. Tesla's new Megacharger in Hodgkins, Illinois, and the 26 Supercharger sites it announced this week are covered in the next section.

IV · New Supercharger & Megacharger Sites

Twenty-six sites, 274 stalls and a Megacharger near Chicago

Three 24-stall stations in Houston and the Las Vegas area led the week; Canada got its northernmost Supercharger, and a Megacharger opened in Hodgkins, Illinois.

Tesla Charging, the company's charging account on X, announced 26 new Supercharger sites from Monday, Nov 9, through Sunday, Nov 15: 21 in the United States with 240 stalls, one in Canada with eight, three in Europe with 22 and one in Japan with four, for 274 stalls by the count of the volunteer-run tracker supercharge.info, which matches every post. All 26 are listed as open, and 25 use V4 posts; the four-stall site at a Dairy Queen in Fort Scott, Kansas, is listed with V3 stalls (supercharge.info).

The largest are three 24-stall stations: at Northbrook Marketplace on West 34th Street in Houston, posted at 10:55 p.m. EST Friday; at Hylo Park South on North Rancho Drive in North Las Vegas, Nevada (post); and at Grand Canyon Village Shopping Center on West Skye Canyon Park Drive in Las Vegas (post). Houston and North Las Vegas charge at up to 500 kW; the Las Vegas site is listed at 325 kW, with five trailer-friendly stalls (supercharge.info).

Four 16-stall sites

Four stations have 16 stalls, all at 500 kW: at a Target on US-22 in Union, New Jersey, where seven stalls are trailer-friendly and every stall has both NACS and CCS1 connectors (supercharge.info); at Hope Station on Blacow Road in Fremont, California (post); at a Dillons on Vine Street in Hays, Kansas, listed as open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. (supercharge.info); and at the visitors center on Padre Boulevard in South Padre Island, Texas (post). In all, 14 of the 26 sites are listed at 500 kW, eight at 325 kW and four at 250 kW.

Canada's northernmost, three voting winners

The eight-stall V4 site at the Shell in Wandering River, Alberta, came online on Tuesday and is now Canada's northernmost Supercharger, taking the title from Grande Prairie until a station under construction in Fort McMurray opens, Drive Tesla reported. Tesla called three sites "Voting Winner Site," picked through its site-suggestion vote: Bellingham, Washington (post); Lake Havasu City, Arizona (post); and four stalls at the ENnoZA store in Izumo, Japan, posted at 10:16 p.m. EST Sunday (post).

Open to other EVs

Six sites list both NACS and CCS1 connectors at every stall: Union; Carbondale, Colorado; Mission, Texas; The Dalles, Oregon; Jonesboro, Arkansas; and Yukon, Oklahoma. Morschwiller-le-Bas, France, and Sunne, Sweden, use CCS2 plugs; Liepāja, Latvia, and Izumo are listed for Teslas only (supercharge.info).

Three sites belong to Supercharger for Business, Tesla's program for stations owned by other businesses, each with four stalls: G & P Development's at The Highlands on East Highland Drive in Jonesboro (post), Francis Energy's at Crest Fresh Market in Yukon (post) and Fort Scott DQ's in Fort Scott (post).

Megacharger

"New Tesla Megacharger: Hodgkins, IL (6 stalls)," Tesla Charging posted at 9:21 p.m. EST Tuesday, linking the site's Tesla page. Hodgkins lies southwest of Chicago. Tesla's third-quarter shareholder update says the company "deployed the first pre-assembled Megacharger to support the Tesla Semi ramp" (Exhibit 99.1).

V · SEC Filings

No new Tesla filings since Friday morning

Capital Group's third-quarter reports show its smallest Tesla stake since early 2025.

No filings for Tesla, Inc. (CIK 0001318605) were accepted on the SEC's Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval system (EDGAR) between 8:00 a.m. EST Friday and 8:47 a.m. EST Monday.

Capital Group trims its stake

Capital Group cut its Tesla holding 7.2% in the third quarter to 51,686,302 shares, its smallest since March 2025, according to eight 13F reports filed on Nov 12 and combined by EV. Most of the reduction came from Capital Research Global Investors, down 27.9% to 6,126,316 shares.
