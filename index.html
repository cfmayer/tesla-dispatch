The Morning Picture

Robotaxi reaches San Antonio as Cybertruck adds a shift

Tesla's Robotaxi service opened in San Antonio at 5 a.m. local time today (6 a.m. EST), across roughly 275 square miles, its largest service area at launch and its eighth city with unsupervised rides, Not a Tesla App reported. The Robotaxi account announced it at 1:27 a.m. EST, then posted at 6:00 a.m. that a bigger Austin service area is live.

Over the weekend Elon Musk said the Cybertruck order backlog is "far more than we can make" before the Texas line is dismantled around June or July next year, and that Tesla will add an extra shift. On Monday he said many deposits will have to be returned. Tesla also confirmed it launches in Argentina on Nov 20, and TSLA rose 4.2% on Friday to $358.22.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

San Antonio opens; Austin grows

Tesla's second new Robotaxi city in a week launches with the largest starting zone yet, while the Austin map expands again.

San Antonio's zone covers all of downtown and nearly everything inside Loop 410, reaching north to Loop 1604 between Interstate 10 and U.S. 281, according to Not a Tesla App, which put Austin at 272 square miles and Houston at 254. It launches with Model Y robotaxis. The city follows Fort Worth, where unsupervised rides began on Nov 2. Tesla named San Antonio, Las Vegas, Phoenix and Jacksonville as its next markets on its third-quarter earnings call.

Tesla gave no details on hours, vehicles or safety monitors for San Antonio, TechCrunch noted. Citing Texas registration data and the Robotaxi Tracker website, it counted more than 400 autonomous vehicles registered in Tesla's Austin fleet, 24 in Fort Worth and five in Miami. Waymo already runs driverless rides in San Antonio.

A 40 kWh Cybercab in liquid silver

"There are efficiency improvements coming that will allow for a 40kWh Cybercab pack. We will make this in liquid silver, so the colors are not too monotonous," Musk wrote at 2:39 p.m. EST Saturday, answering a post comparing battery use in a robotaxi with a consumer vehicle. The Cybercab now in Austin service has a 48 kWh pack, Teslarati reported. Not a Tesla App counts more than 300 Cybercabs running in Austin, with Dallas due this month.

Quick Hit

Blind-spot save. "FSD Supervised is looking out for you," Tesla posted at 3:51 p.m. EST Sunday, sharing an owner's clip of the system braking hard when an SUV merged without checking its blind spot, then resuming once it cleared.

II · Sales & Demand

Argentina on Nov 20; Model Y leads Australia

Tesla sets its Argentine debut at a Buenos Aires-area mall; the Model Y passes the Toyota RAV4 in Australia's year-to-date count.

"Llegamos a Argentina el 20 de Noviembre" ("We arrive in Argentina on November 20"), Tesla's Latin America account posted at 7:00 a.m. EST Friday with a video of a Model Y in Buenos Aires. "Launching in Argentina later this month," the main Tesla account added at 10:02 a.m. La Nación reported that Tesla is preparing a space on level 1 of the Unicenter mall in Martínez showing two vehicles, plus a test-drive area in the parking lot with a fleet of ten cars.

Argentina raised the maximum free-on-board value of fully electric cars that can enter without the 35% import duty from $16,000 to $35,000 as of Oct 1, and in August began accepting vehicles certified to U.S. federal safety standards, the paper said. Tesla has not published Argentine prices.

Model Y tops RAV4 in Australia

The Model Y sold 39,035 units in Australia through October, ahead of the Toyota RAV4's 35,927, making it the country's best-selling passenger vehicle so far this year, CarsGuide reported. Its 8,072 deliveries in June were the highest monthly sales since the Holden Commodore in the early 2000s, the site said. "Tesla Model Y is the best-selling car of any kind on Earth," Musk wrote at 10:50 a.m. EST Friday, quote-posting the Australian figures.

III · Energy & Charging

Court deems Tesla liable for fraud in Solar Roof case

A San Diego judge imposed the issue sanction after repeated discovery failures; Tesla is seeking appellate review. New Supercharger sites are in Section IV.

A California judge has deemed Tesla liable for fraud and negligent misrepresentation in Conroy v. Tesla, a Solar Roof lawsuit filed in 2022 in San Diego Superior Court, after the company repeatedly failed to produce evidence, Electrek reported Saturday from the court orders. Judge Cynthia Freeland sanctioned Tesla $15,467.50 in February and $13,500 in June over discovery, then on Sep 11 limited the trial on those two claims to compensatory and punitive damages. She denied reconsideration on Oct 27.

Tesla had already stipulated to liability on three of the five claims, including breach of contract and negligence. The roughly $110,000 roof with six Powerwalls, contracted in 2020, never passed county inspection or produced power; the homeowner puts repairs at "well over $2.5 million," and Tesla offered $5,000 in April 2022. In an Oct 29 filing Tesla asked to pause the case while it petitions the California Court of Appeal, calling the order "unduly punitive." The Nov 20 trial date is off; a trial readiness conference is set for Dec 4.

Wall Connector for Business adds 62 sites

Tesla Charging listed 62 Wall Connector for Business sites opened in the past two weeks, with 376 Wall Connectors on public and private properties, at 1:31 p.m. EST Friday. The largest were 42 at Vinton Avenue in Culver City, California; 23 at The Leon in Millburn, New Jersey; and 20 at Hollywood Aurora Casino and Hotel in Aurora, Illinois. Two sites were in Thailand.

Quick Hit

Smart Charging. With Powerwall and Wall Connector, Smart Charging weighs solar output, home goals and driving needs to time EV charging, with a Savings Mode that factors in solar production and utility rates, Tesla Energy posted at 3:00 p.m. EST Friday.

IV · New Supercharger & Megacharger Sites

Sixteen sites, 155 stalls, two Megachargers

Four 16-stall stations in Arlington, Santa Ana, North Brunswick and Veszprém led the week; two Megachargers went live at the Port of Long Beach.

Tesla Charging, the company's charging account on X, announced 16 new Supercharger sites from Monday, Nov 2, through Sunday, Nov 8: six in the United States with 84 stalls, one in Canada with eight, six in Europe and Turkey with 52, and three in Asia with 11, for 155 stalls by the count of the volunteer-run tracker supercharge.info. All 16 are listed as open, and every stall is a V4 post. Tesla's post for The Colony, Texas, said eight stalls; the tracker lists 12 (supercharge.info).

The largest are four 16-stall stations: at a Steak 'n Shake on East Lamar Boulevard in Arlington, Texas, posted at 6:18 p.m. EST Friday; at Grand Avenue Plaza on North Grand Avenue in Santa Ana, California (post); at the Brunswick Shopping Center on Milltown Road in North Brunswick Township, New Jersey (post); and in Veszprém, Hungary (post). Arlington and Santa Ana are the week's only 500 kW sites; Arlington is listed as open 10 a.m. to midnight (supercharge.info).

The 325 kW sites

Five sites are listed at 325 kW: North Brunswick Township; The Colony, Texas, at an Exxon; Castaic, California, where two of 12 stalls at Vine at Castaic are trailer-friendly (supercharge.info); Chicago; and Gatineau, Quebec, where eight stalls at Plaza Sotramont opened as a "Voting Winner Site" (post). The other nine charge at up to 250 kW.

Open to other EVs

Two sites list both NACS and CCS1 connectors at every stall: North Brunswick Township and Chicago. The sites in Spier, Netherlands; Oberkrämer, Germany; Basingstoke and Basildon, United Kingdom; Veszprém; and Konyaaltı in Antalya, Turkey, use CCS2 plugs. Petaling Jaya, Malaysia, and Tampines Mall in Singapore are listed for Teslas only.

Two sites belong to Supercharger for Business, Tesla's program for stations owned by other businesses: EVgo's 12 stalls on South Ashland Avenue in Chicago (post) and EV OTM's four in Basildon (post).

Megachargers

"Semi for Business private sites give Semi buyers the best total cost of ownership and experience. 2 Forum Megachargers are now operational in the Port of Long Beach, CA," Tesla Charging posted at 3:07 p.m. EST Friday, pointing to its Megacharger for Business page. Tesla's third-quarter shareholder update says the company "deployed the first pre-assembled Megacharger to support the Tesla Semi ramp" (Exhibit 99.1).

V · Cybertruck, Optimus & Terafab

Extra Cybertruck shift before the line comes out

Musk says orders outrun output for the truck's final months; at the Baron conference he set out a research chip fab at Giga Texas and a rental model for Optimus.

"The Tesla Cybertruck order backlog is currently far more than we can make before dismantling the line for Cybercab expansion around June/July next year. We will add an extra shift to try to get all orders done in time," Elon Musk wrote at 1:12 p.m. EST Saturday. Earlier he told a reader Tesla is "still building Cybertruck for another 8 or 9 months."

At 2:20 a.m. EST Monday he went further: "The Cybertruck order rate far exceeds our production rate. We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people. This is not a case of Cybertruck being unprofitable, as has been misreported by legacy media, but rather that the opportunity cost of the complexity of Cybertruck is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production" (post). U.S. Cybertruck sales fell to about 20,000 last year from nearly 39,000 in 2024, according to Kelley Blue Book, Reuters reported.

Terafab: a research fab in Austin

Speaking virtually to Ron Baron at the Baron Investment Conference in New York on Friday, Musk said Tesla is building a research fabrication facility at Gigafactory Texas to try new chip-making processes, with successful ones moving to the larger Terafab plant planned for Grimes County, Texas, TeslaNorth reported. He said the research fab could cut chip-development work that normally takes a year to about a month. The long-run goal is one terawatt of AI chips a year. "If you want to iterate fast with radically new chip designs, join the Terafab project!" he posted at 6:53 p.m. EST Friday.

Optimus: rent, not sell

Asked whether Tesla would sell or rent its humanoid robots, Musk said renting would probably be preferred, and set a minimum expectation of five times a human's weekly productivity by running continuously, per TeslaNorth. He estimated that 20 million to 30 million sufficiently capable humanoid robots could in theory match the productive output of the United States, and said robots might at first cost about as much as cars to make. Tesla has not announced an Optimus ordering timeline.

Board letter urges shareholders to vote

A Friday proxy supplement asks retail investors to back Musk, Robyn Denholm and JB Straubel ahead of the Dec. 3 annual meeting.
