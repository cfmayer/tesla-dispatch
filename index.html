The Morning Picture

Tesla unveils Powercore, its own grid transformer

Tesla's Megapack team used the RE+ energy show in Las Vegas on Tuesday to introduce Powercore, a solid-state unit that combines a medium-voltage transformer, an inverter and a circuit breaker in one enclosure. "7.4 MW/9 MVA continuously at 34.5 kVac w/ grid-forming support for 10MVA of peak power," the Tesla Megapack account posted at 11:00 a.m. EST. "Made in America. Operating 2027." Tesla is pitching it for battery storage, solar farms and AI data centers, and says it is working with Nvidia on 800-volt direct-current power for data centers.

Elsewhere, Tesla added a record 200 Cybercabs to the Texas list of driverless vehicles, taking its fleet there past 1,500, and opened its first Megacharger for the Semi in Texas. Software update 2026.44.1 began reaching cars. TSLA rose 0.1% to $380.11 on Tuesday as the S&P 500 fell 1.0%.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

A record 200 Cybercabs join the Texas fleet

Tesla now has more registered driverless vehicles in Texas than Waymo; federal crash data list eight Cybercab incidents.

The Texas Department of Motor Vehicles listed 1,507 vehicles under Tesla Robotaxi LLC's automated-vehicle authorization on Tuesday evening, up from 1,306, after Tesla added 200 Cybercabs and one Model Y, its largest one-day addition so far, EV (eletric-vehicles.com) reported. All 200 new Cybercabs carry 2027 model-year vehicle identification numbers with the plant code for Tesla's Austin factory. The registry now lists 773 Cybercabs and 734 Model Ys, so the two-seater without a steering wheel or pedals makes up 51.3% of the Texas fleet. The previous one-day record was 150 Cybercabs on Oct 8.

The batch put Tesla back ahead of Alphabet's Waymo on the Texas list: Waymo had 1,321 registered vehicles on Tuesday, according to RobotaxiTracker data cited by EV, so Tesla now leads by 186. Tesla's Robotaxi service runs around the clock in eight cities, five of them in Texas (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio). Tesla has opened Cybercab orders to select customers at $29,990 plus a $299 monthly autonomy subscription, with deliveries estimated for December.

Eight Cybercab incidents in federal crash data

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's (NHTSA) latest crash-report release, covering submissions through Oct 15, lists the Cybercab in eight incident reports, up from one, Drive Tesla reported. Four are in NHTSA's automated driving system data, all in Austin: the August pothole encounter, two September cases in which, by Tesla's descriptions, another driver struck a stopped Cybercab, and one in which a Cybercab carrying a passenger crossed a concrete boundary and a sidewalk and went down a curb at about 3 mph. None of the four lists an injury.

The other four are in the Level 2 driver-assistance data, meaning a test driver was on board: in Austin (a minor injury with hospitalization), Philadelphia (a highway collision at about 53 mph, injury status unknown), Lehi, Utah (a minor injury) and Tampa, Florida (no injuries). Tesla withheld the narratives of all four as confidential. NHTSA also has an audit open, since Sep 3, into how Tesla certified that the Cybercab meets federal safety standards without conventional controls; it has not announced a finding, EV noted.

Quick Hit

Waymo leaves the freeway. Waymo has stopped offering freeway rides in all its markets after California's Department of Motor Vehicles barred its driverless cars from the state's freeways from Nov 11, citing "recurring incursions" into active construction zones; outside California the pause is Waymo's own decision, EV reported.

Update 2026.44.1 carries FSD v14.3.13

Software update 2026.44.1, which began reaching a small first wave of cars on Tuesday, lists FSD (Supervised) v14.3.13 and Automatic Collision Evasion in its release notes, TeslaNorth reported. Neither is new: v14.3.13 began reaching AI4 cars in October with notes on parking, driver monitoring and unusual road situations, and Automatic Collision Evasion can engage FSD during manual driving if the system detects inattention and a possibly unintended disengagement. FSD still requires an attentive driver.

Quick Hit

Uber rides in Munich. Uber and the Chinese self-driving company Momenta began free, supervised robotaxi rides in northern Munich on Wednesday, with a safety operator on every trip under test permits from Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA), the first autonomous rides offered through the Uber app in Germany, EV reported.

II · Software & Vehicles

A cleaner charging map and an "SI Computer"

Update 2026.44.1 groups Supercharger pins and reorganizes the Service menu; Cybercab buyers will rely on DC fast charging.

The headline feature of 2026.44.1 is Charger Network View: instead of crowding the navigation map with overlapping pins, nearby Superchargers are grouped into a single pin with a number, and zooming in shows the individual sites, Not a Tesla App reported. It is available on all vehicles, including Intel and AMD cars and legacy Model S and Model X. The Service menu under Controls now has two tabs: Maintenance (tire pressures, recommended maintenance, battery health) and Actions (Car Wash Mode, Wiper Service Mode, camera previews, headlight adjustment).

The car's AI Computer is now called the SI Computer in the vehicle information screen, a terminology change that follows Tesla's owner's manuals, which made the switch in October. The firmware also hints at a new navigation option and Grok wake-word and chat-history improvements that do not appear to be live yet. Not a Tesla App called it a light release ahead of next month's holiday update.

The Cybercab can't charge at home yet

The Cybercab has no onboard charger, which Tesla confirmed at its Austin launch event in September, so it can take only direct-current fast charging, Electrek reported. That rules out a Wall Connector, a Mobile Connector or a Level 2 public station. The order agreement limits the car to NACS DC fast charging, Superchargers included, and says only the registered owner may use it while physically present for the whole trip; the subscription agreement lets the car drive itself unoccupied only to a service center.

Tesla has shown a wireless charging pad charging the robotaxi at 25 kW, and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) granted it a waiver in February to use ultra-wideband radios to guide the car onto the pad, but Tesla has never priced or dated the pad. In Austin, Tesla has filed plans for a Cybercab hub on St. Elmo Road with 48 V4 Supercharger stalls in a first phase and cabinets for 80 wireless chargers in a second, Electrek noted.

Patent office reviews four charging patents

The US Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) agreed to review four of the six charging patents that ChargeLogic LLC of Midland, Texas, is asserting against Tesla in federal court, and rejected Tesla's challenges to the other two, EV reported from a USPTO notice. ChargeLogic sued in March, claiming that charging features in every Tesla model from the Model 3 to the Semi and the Cybercab infringe. One patent under review covers inductive charging, which ChargeLogic's complaint ties to the Cybercab's wireless charging and positioning system. The infringement case continues in the Western District of Texas meanwhile.

III · Energy & Charging

Powercore takes aim at the transformer shortage

One box replaces two conversion stages; Tesla says Nvidia is working with it on 800-volt data-center power.

Powercore accepts direct current from 800 to 2,000 volts and connects to alternating-current networks from 10 to 34.5 kilovolts, with a peak efficiency of 98.86%, an operating range of −40°C to 55°C and warranties of up to 25 years, Not a Tesla App reported. Its built-in solid-state breaker can interrupt a fault in 100 microseconds, about 1,000 times faster than conventional equipment, and its grid-forming controls help regulate grid voltage and frequency. Tesla has not given a price, a launch customer or a factory location.

"We've done 3 terawatts of power converters at Tesla. We build all of our power converters in-house, and we wanted to use that capability to provide a bankable and reliable pathway for customers to confidently adopt what is fundamentally a new technology to the industry."

AJ Booth, head of energy and charging, Tesla, to pv magazine USA, Nov 17

Shortages of transformer materials have pushed lead times for conventional units to as long as four years, slowing grid upgrades and renewable projects, pv magazine USA noted. For Megapack sites, Powercore connects to the batteries over a direct-current bus and replaces the current setup, in which Megapack inverters put out 480 volts before a separate transformer steps it up; Tesla says the old architecture had twice the losses, Electrek reported. For solar farms it works as a central inverter for 1,500-volt arrays and coming 2,000-volt designs.

Straight to 800 volts for AI racks

For data centers, Powercore takes medium-voltage grid power to 800 volts DC in one step, and Tesla says cutting conversion losses would let operators run more graphics processors on the same grid connection, TeslaNorth reported. Tesla is working to qualify it under Nvidia's DSX Ready program, under which Megapack was among the first battery systems qualified, and says it already has 10 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of Megapack storage at data centers. It estimates that in many regions a 100-megawatt data center could be powered by 500 MW of solar and 1.7 GWh of batteries, a proposed configuration rather than a customer project.

Musk: planned for three years

Powercore's first units are due to operate in 2027, the same year Heron Power, the solid-state transformer startup founded by Drew Baglino, Tesla's former senior vice president of powertrain and energy, plans pilot installations and volume production of its Heron Link, Electrek reported. Heron has named customers, including Vantage, Intersect and RWE, and is building a factory in Morgan Hill, California. When an X user (@wholemars) wrote that Powercore "seems to compete directly" with Heron, Musk replied at 12:54 p.m. EST: "No, this has been planned for 3+ years." Tesla first said it would build its own transformers in September 2025, when it unveiled Megapack 3 and Megablock.

First Megacharger in Texas

Tesla opened an eight-stall Megacharger for the Semi in Mesquite, just east of Dallas, Tesla Charging posted at 8:04 p.m. EST Tuesday; each stall delivers up to 1.2 megawatts, Drive Tesla reported. "We're rapidly scaling charging & service for Semi in the state," the Tesla Semi account wrote. It follows last week's openings in Hodgkins, Illinois; Urbandale, Iowa; Ogallala, Nebraska; and Riverdale, Utah, and Tesla recently opened its first dedicated Semi service center in Laredo, Texas.

Four new Supercharger sites

Tesla Charging also announced Superchargers in Barstow, California, at 1540 East Main Street with 16 stalls (post); Spanish Fort, Alabama, with 8 (post); North Conway, New Hampshire, on White Mountain Highway with 16 (post); and Vegamót on Iceland's Snæfellsnes peninsula with 6 (post), 46 stalls in all.

Solar output passes Tesla's own use

"Customer solar systems deployed by @TeslaEnergy now generate more than 2x the electricity that our global operations consume each year," Tesla posted at 5:03 p.m. EST Tuesday. The company did not give the figures behind the comparison.

IV · Chips & People

Texas A&M lines up a workforce for Terafab

A $205.5 million semiconductor institute is about 20% built; a former Tesla energy director moves to Nuro.

Texas A&M's new semiconductor institute will help train workers for Terafab, the chip fab Tesla and SpaceX broke ground on in Grimes County in August, institute director Steve Putna told the Brazos Valley Intergovernmental Committee on Nov 16, according to local broadcaster KBTX, as reported by TeslaNorth. David Staack, the Texas A&M System's deputy vice chancellor for research, said the institute was conceived before Terafab and would serve other regional companies too. The building is about 20% complete, with substantial completion expected around the end of 2027 and significant operations in mid-2028; the A&M System has budgeted $205.5 million for the institute, its infrastructure and equipment.

Quick Hit

Nuro hires from Tesla Energy. Self-driving company Nuro named Neha Rao, a former Tesla director who oversaw service operations for its solar, storage and charging fleets, as head of autonomy operations, in charge of the teams that gather data to train its driving system, EV reported.

V · SEC Filings

Optimus asks shareholders to vote

A new proxy filing carries Tesla's robot-narrated voting guide; Goldman Sachs nearly tripled its Tesla call options in the third quarter.

Tesla filed additional proxy materials (DEFA14A) at 5:15 p.m. EST Tuesday containing posts that Tesla and Optimus made on X that day, with a transcript of the video, and an updated VoteTesla.com site, according to the filing. In the video, Optimus asks holders to "vote with the Board's recommendations on all proposals, including the re-election of Elon Musk, Robyn Denholm, and JB Straubel," and walks through voting online, by QR code or by phone. Most shareholders must vote by 11:59 p.m. Eastern on Dec 2. Enrolling in Tesla's new Voluntary Retail Voting Program, which votes shares with the board at future meetings, does not cast a vote this year.

The Dec 3 meeting, at 4:00 p.m. EST, also votes on 2025 executive pay (non-binding) and PricewaterhouseCoopers as auditor, TeslaNorth reported.

Goldman Sachs loads up on calls

Goldman Sachs' Tesla call options covered 5,662,800 underlying shares on Sep 30, up 186.5% from June and the most since the end of 2022, while its puts rose 95.8% to 5,242,800 shares, according to its 13F filed on Monday, EV reported. Calls outnumbered puts for the first time in Goldman's 13Fs since at least September 2020. Goldman cut its Tesla shareholding 11.7% to 27,421,297 shares. As a dealer, Goldman may hold options to serve or hedge client trades, and a 13F does not show strikes, expiries or whether options were bought or sold.
