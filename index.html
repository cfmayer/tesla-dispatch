The Morning Picture

Cybertruck yields floor space to Cybercab

Elon Musk said overnight that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027 "to make room for increased Cybercab production," in a post at 1:30 a.m. EST Friday. He later told a reader the Cybertruck fleet "will be around for a long time." Cybertruck Lead Engineer Wes Morrill said spare parts and service will continue. CNBC noted Musk did not say whether truck assembly moves elsewhere; Texas is the only known plant equipped for the stainless-steel pickup.

Hours earlier, Tesla raised U.S. Cybertruck stickers again and paused new orders for the Dual-Motor all-wheel-drive trim. TSLA fell 10.8% on Thursday as broader markets sold off. Separately, Musk amplified South Korea data showing Tesla still No. 1 among imported brands for a ninth month, and said Terafab "is moving fast."

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Cybercab capacity first; NHTSA answers stay sealed

Musk's mid-2027 plant shift points floor space at the purpose-built robotaxi; Tesla's sworn Cybercab certification reply to NHTSA remains confidential.

Musk's overnight note frames Gigafactory Texas as trading Cybertruck volume for more Cybercabs, the two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals that Tesla already builds there beside Model Y. Paid Cybercab rides began in Austin on Sep 3; the car is open to the public only there so far, with Model Y Robotaxis also running unsupervised in Texas and Florida cities. About two dozen gold Cybercabs have been winter-testing in Metro Detroit with temporary wheels and human drivers, The Detroit News reported Wednesday. Dallas service is due later this month, per Not a Tesla App.

NHTSA docket: certification under seal

Tesla met the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's Oct 30 deadline for sworn answers under Special Order AQ26002 on how Cybercab complies with federal motor vehicle safety standards, then asked that the entire response stay confidential, Electrek reported Thursday from the public docket. The only substantive public filing is Tesla's Cybercab First Responder Interaction Plan. NHTSA has not ruled on the confidentiality request and has not found Cybercab noncompliant. Zoox took a public Part 555 exemption route for its wheel-less robotaxi; Tesla self-certified.

Europe tour under way

Tesla's Cybercab Europe tour opened Thursday in Zurich, according to Tesla's events page.

II · Sales & Demand

Korea still No. 1; Cybertruck stickers climb

October imports keep Tesla atop KAIDA's brand table for a ninth month; U.S. Cybertruck prices rise again as Dual-Motor orders pause.

Tesla registered 9,165 imported passenger cars in South Korea in October, remaining the top imported brand for a ninth straight month since February, the Korea Automobile Importers & Distributors Association reported via Yonhap. That was up 110.7% from 4,350 a year earlier and down 25.9% from September on supply shortages, KAIDA said. Model Y Premium led all imported models; Model 3 ranked third. Musk quote-posted a summary of the run at 1:15 a.m. EST Friday as "Tesla Korea."

Cybertruck prices up; Dual-Motor paused

On Tesla's U.S. Cybertruck design page, Premium All-Wheel Drive listed at $92,490 (from $84,990) and Cyberbeast at $102,490 (from $99,990), while Dual-Motor All-Wheel Drive showed $82,490 but grayed out for new orders, TeslaNorth reported Thursday evening. The Premium jump is $7,500, or about 9%, on top of an August increase. Tesla had not posted a separate product note explaining the change.

III · Energy & Charging

EVgo's first Supercharger, plus Canberra Megapacks

Twelve stalls open on Chicago's South Ashland Avenue under Supercharger for Business; Williamsdale's 136 Megapacks go live.

@TeslaCharging posted at 3:15 p.m. EST Thursday a new EVgo Supercharger with 12 stalls on South Ashland Avenue in Chicago, sold through Tesla's Supercharger for Business program. A four-stall EV OTM site also opened in Basildon, United Kingdom, at 12:30 p.m. EST (post).

Canberra's Big Battery online

The ACT Government and Eku Energy switched on the Williamsdale battery energy storage system on Thursday, calling it the largest in the Territory's history: 250 megawatts from 136 Tesla Megapack 2 units, enough to supply about one-third of Canberra's electricity needs for up to two hours at peak, per the ACT media release.

IV · Cybertruck, Cybercab & Terafab

Mid-2027 wind-down, parts stay, fab accelerates

Musk and Morrill clarify the truck's runway and owner support; Musk says Terafab is moving fast.

"Tesla will be winding down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production," Elon Musk wrote at 1:30 a.m. EST Friday. Asked about an "end of an era" reaction, he replied at 3:57 a.m. EST: "True, but the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time" (post). CNBC and TeslaNorth both noted he did not say whether production ends entirely or moves.

Morrill: spare parts and service continue

Wes Morrill wrote at 1:58 a.m. EST Friday that Cybertruck "was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab," thanking contributors and owners. Asked whether owners would keep long-lasting support, he replied at 2:36 a.m. EST: "Yes, of course! Tesla will maintain spare parts and service will continue support" (post).

Terafab "moving fast"

At 3:25 a.m. EST Friday Musk quote-posted coverage of Tesla's chip effort and wrote, "Terafab is moving fast" (post).
