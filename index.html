The Morning Picture

Six million miles between major collisions

Teslas driven with FSD (Supervised) went 6,006,200 miles per major collision in North America over the latest 12 months, against 2,031,221 miles for Teslas driven manually with active safety features, 845,915 without them and a U.S. average of 660,164, according to Tesla's updated safety page, which Sawyer Merritt posted at 11:37 p.m. EDT Monday. Tesla counts a collision against FSD if the system was active within five seconds of impact, does not assign fault and updates the figures quarterly. The page's counter has passed 16.7 billion FSD miles, more than 6.6 billion of them on city streets.

The period covers about 9.48 billion FSD miles and 1,579 major collisions, and the minor-collision rate was one every 1,760,823 miles against 685,390 for manual driving with active safety, Tesla Mag reported; China is not included. Tesla's fleet now drives "an average of 1,000 miles every 1.8 seconds on FSD, or 50M miles per day," Merritt wrote at 11:19 p.m. The company's safety claims for the software "are highly contested by safety researchers," Reuters noted on Tuesday.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Turo renters can book FSD from Wednesday

The car-sharing marketplace will check FSD status directly through Tesla's API, and hosts get a free month.

Starting October 28, U.S. guests on Turo can search for and book Teslas with FSD (Supervised) activated, with the status verified through a direct Tesla API integration, the company said in a release on Monday. Eligible hosts receive a free one-month FSD trial. Renters see cautionary language and an optional Tesla tutorial, and listings can carry a "Self-Driving" label, TechCrunch reported.

"Our own research shows that 55% of Americans are interested in driverless technology for travel, growing to 71% for Gen Z," Turo chief executive Andre Haddad said (post, 9:25 a.m. EDT Monday). The Model 3 and Model Y are Turo's third- and fourth-most-popular vehicles this year, TechCrunch said.

Houston's map grows tenfold

Robotaxi's Houston zone now covers 254 square miles, up 916% on Monday, behind Austin's 272 and ahead of Dallas at 81, Miami at 31, Orlando at 26 and Tampa at 24, Merritt posted at 12:02 p.m. EDT. Riders can now hail cars across much of the 610 Loop, though part of its northeast section falls outside the rocket-shaped map, Houston Public Media reported. "Also a very useful geofence," AI chief Ashok Elluswamy wrote at 10:59 a.m.

Quick Hit

Eyes on the barrier. On a Reuters test drive in Bratislava, owner Igor Marcek had to brake by hand when the car did not slow for a closed barrier. "You really have to be awake there," he said. Slovakia approved Tesla Assisted Driving this month; it costs €99 a month (Reuters via TimesLIVE).

II · Sales & Demand

Barclays sees deliveries growing again

After two straight annual declines, the bank forecasts 1.80 million vehicles this year and 1.92 million in 2027.

Tesla is on track to grow deliveries this year after two straight annual declines, Barclays analyst Dan Levy wrote in Monday's upgrade, forecasting 1.80 million vehicles in 2026 and 1.92 million in 2027, Yahoo Finance reported. FSD was on more than 75% of North American deliveries in the third quarter, up about 20 percentage points from the second, and exports from Giga Shanghai now make up about 25% to 30% of global volume, the note said.

"We believe the improvement in Tesla's core business + inflection in Robotaxi, alongside the potential premium from a combination with SPCX, offers attractive risk/reward for the stock," Levy wrote (Yahoo Finance). Barclays sees an all-stock purchase of Tesla by SpaceX as the most likely structure, and its $250 downside case assumes no deal.

Quick Hit

1.7 million buyers. About 1.7 million customers worldwide have paid for FSD, TechCrunch reported, citing Tesla.

III · Energy & Charging

Powerwall 3P reaches Dutch homes

Installations have begun in the Netherlands, and the three-phase battery can now drive SG-Ready heat pumps.

"Powerwall 3P installations have begun in the Netherlands and can now integrate with your SG-Ready heat pump," Tesla Energy posted in Dutch at 10:32 a.m. EDT Monday (translated). Tesla opened sales in August of the three-phase battery, built for the Dutch market, with 15.4 kilowatts of continuous output and 21 kilowatts at peak, Solar Magazine reported. Tesla says that is enough to fully power more than 95% of Dutch homes during an outage.

On the grid side, management has said Tesla is nearly sold out of Megapacks for 2027 on strong data-center demand, and Barclays expects deployments to grow at least 50% next year as Megafactory Houston ramps, Yahoo Finance reported.

Seven new Supercharger sites

Tesla Charging announced seven sites with 53 stalls since Monday afternoon: Bedminster, New Jersey, eight stalls (1:36 p.m. EDT); Surprise, Arizona, 11 (2:59 p.m.); Tillamook, Oregon, eight (5:19 p.m.); Reynoldsburg, Ohio, eight (6:55 p.m.); Auckland South, New Zealand, six (10:23 p.m.); Pearl Bangkok in Thailand, four (10:28 p.m.); and Walla Walla, Washington, eight, a "Voting Winner Site" picked by public vote (12:09 a.m. Tuesday).

IV · Cybercab

The line is ahead of the road

Tesla's engineering chief says the Cybercab line can build cars faster than Robotaxi can deploy them.

"We don't run every day because the line can run so fast, it's currently outpacing our deployments," Tesla vice president of vehicle engineering Lars Moravy said in a Munro Live tour of the Texas line published Monday, calling it "a bit of a race right now," TeslaNorth reported. "Right now, the manufacturing line's ahead." The Robotaxi account shared the video at 1:25 p.m. EDT as a "Cybercab engineering deep dive" (post).

Final assembly takes 25 automated stations, against five to 10 times that on a typical Model Y line, and the whole line is about 78% automated, working toward a 10-second cycle time, engineers told Munro Live. There is no paint shop; body panels get their color in the molding machine, and the car is built to "run half a million miles at least."

Inside the pack

The 4680 pack sits in a steel enclosure, uses wireless battery management and drops the pyrotechnic fuse for fast contactors. Tesla's battery director graded it an "A minus," against a "D" for the Model Y structural pack and a "B minus" for Cybertruck, TeslaNorth said. Musk quote-posted a Cybercab post at 12:24 a.m. EDT Tuesday with "A Storm of Cybercabs" (post).

Potting cells module by module lets service crews replace a single failed module, "worth, you know, 1,500 bucks a piece," instead of the pack; swapping a whole pack before half a million miles "is a killer on cost," the battery director said. Each battery-management board now carries a radio chip that adds 20 to 30 cents but deletes a harness worth about $1 plus roughly 50 cents of assembly (TeslaNorth).

More cabs in Austin

Tesla sent more than 50 Cybercabs to the Austin F1 weekend, and downtown "you literally will see four cabs in a matter of 30 seconds no matter what direction you look," an engineer said on the tour (TeslaNorth). "Austin's robot population is growing," the Robotaxi account posted at 1:40 p.m. EDT Monday. Replying to a post that said the battle for autonomous rides in Austin might already be over, Musk wrote at 12:03 a.m. Tuesday, "It was never a real contest" (post).

Quick Hit

"Not yet." That was the Tesla team's answer when Munro Live asked when it could get a Cybercab to tear down (TeslaNorth).
