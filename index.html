The Morning Picture

Latvia makes ten

"Now approved in Latvia. Rollout begins soon," Tesla Europe (@teslaeurope) posted at 6:36 a.m. EDT Monday. Latvia's Road Traffic Safety Directorate (CSDD) said it has recognized the Dutch vehicle authority's (RDW) provisional EU type approval of the driver-assistance system, called Tesla Assisted Driving in Europe and previously Full Self-Driving (Supervised), Apollo.lv reported. The decision weighed changes Tesla described: a lower maximum threshold for exceeding the speed limit, a manually set speed offset that resets after every trip, and stronger driver monitoring.

The CSDD describes a Level 2 system: hands need not stay on the wheel, but the driver must be ready to take over and may not use a phone; snow, mud and road salt can interfere, and on gravel roads it may work only partly (Apollo.lv). Latvia is the tenth EU country to back the Dutch ruling; Tesla filed there on April 13, and the subscription costs €99 a month, EV reported. Reuters also counted Latvia as the tenth.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Cybercabs crowd the F1 weekend

Austin sightings hit a record on race Saturday as the Texas registry neared 1,000 Robotaxi vehicles.

The volunteer tracker Cybercab Spotter counted a record 1,250 Tesla Robotaxi sightings in Austin on Saturday, 1,159 of them driverless, or 93%, topping the previous high of 1,072 on October 16, EV reported. The service area now reaches the Circuit of the Americas for the Formula 1 race weekend, and ride requests showed "Last call is 1am · Limited to nearby dropoffs" (Teslarati).

"Robotaxis as far as the eye can see!" Ashok Elluswamy (@aelluswamy), who leads Tesla's AI software team, posted at 3:48 p.m. EDT Sunday; at 3:07 a.m. EDT Monday he added "A Storm of Cybercabs." Sawyer Merritt (@SawyerMerritt) posted at 1:45 p.m. EDT Sunday: "Over 50 Tesla Cybercabs spotted so far dropping off passengers today at the F1 race in Austin, Texas."

Registry count

Texas records showed 970 Tesla Robotaxi vehicles, 463 of them Cybercabs, at midday Sunday, according to EV and Teslarati. Merritt's own tally of Texas DMV records was 465 Cybercabs after 43 registrations on Saturday (post, 12:56 a.m. EDT Sunday); the two counts differ by two. "Tesla is nearing 1,000 robotaxis registered in Texas to date," he posted at 1:12 a.m. EDT Monday.

Quick Hit

Same notes, new build. Software 2026.38.5.11 began rolling out on Saturday with FSD (Supervised) v14.3.13 for AI4 cars and v14.3 Lite for Hardware 3; "This is an updated version of FSD v14.3.13. The release notes remain the same." It reached 8.7% of cars tracked by TeslaFi, Not a Tesla App shows.

Riders can now bring up the rearview camera during a Cybercab ride, and a new quiet mode appears to darken the cabin display, Merritt posted at 10:17 a.m. EDT Saturday.

Ten approvals, and a December vote

The ten countries that have accepted the system hold about 14% of the EU's population, by a tally from Roland Pircher cited by EV. An EU-wide approval needs 15 of the 27 member states representing 65% of the population. The Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) did not vote on October 6, and Germany's Transport Ministry says a vote is planned for December; Germany, France, Italy and Spain, about 58% of the population, have not recognized the Dutch approval (EV).

A lighting rulebook for cars without a wheel

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) proposed updating the federal lighting standard, FMVSS No. 108, for vehicles driven only by an automated driving system, with performance rules for optional automated-vehicle marker lamps, it announced Friday. "These proposed updates address unnecessary regulatory burdens for vehicles built without traditional manual controls while establishing clear performance standards for automated vehicle marker lamps," Administrator Jonathan Morrison said.

The notice appeared in the Federal Register on Monday; comments on docket NHTSA-2026-1519 close November 25. Vehicles without manual controls would be exempt from the rules that turn signals cancel with the steering wheel and by hand; "Unlike a human driver, the system cannot forget that the turn signals are active," NHTSA wrote. The lamps would be steady cyan, four to eight of them at least 15 inches high, EV reported. The notice does not name Tesla, but it matches a point in NHTSA's September 10 special order on the Cybercab, whose answers are due October 30; cars running FSD (Supervised) could not light the lamps (EV).

II · Sales & Demand

Giga Berlin builds 7,000 Model Ys in a week

The plant is about 500 cars a week short of its 7,500 target.

"Ramp-up is in full swing at Giga Berlin. This week we hit the next production milestone: 7,000 Model Y built in one week," Tesla's manufacturing account said on Friday, EV reported. "Making 7,000 cars in a single week is hard work for the entire team! And we will keep ramping from here. Job is not done!" plant manager André Thierig wrote, and in a reply described the plant's schedule as "24/5."

That is about 1,400 cars per production day and leaves the plant about 500 a week short of the 7,500 target management set for October, EV noted. Tesla's German registrations more than tripled in September to 12,552, and Tesla plans to file in December for a second production hall of about 536,000 square meters, the outlet said, citing the broadcaster rbb.

III · Energy & Charging

Megapack output "ramping up rapidly"

Tesla also pitched Powerwall 3 as an add-on for Powerwall 2 owners.

"By the way, Tesla ramping up Megapack production rapidly to meet increasing demand," Elon Musk (@elonmusk) wrote in a reply at 1:09 p.m. EDT Friday. On last week's call he said Tesla is "almost sold out of all Megapacks for 2027," as quoted in a Canaccord note that Sawyer Merritt posted.

"Powerwall 3 delivers 11.5 kW of continuous power, nearly double the power of Powerwall 2," Tesla Energy (@teslaenergy) posted at 11:52 a.m. EDT Friday, adding that one added to an existing Powerwall 2 system can "Extend your backup duration during outages." This week's new Supercharger sites are listed in the next section.

Wall Connectors. Tesla Charging listed 60 Wall Connector for Business sites, with 419 Wall Connectors across public and private properties, that opened in the past two weeks (12:42 p.m. EDT Friday).

IV · New Supercharger & Megacharger Sites

Sixteen sites, 178 stalls

Two 24-stall stations in Houston and Deerfield Beach led the week; three U.S. sites charge at up to 500 kW. No new Megacharger site was posted.

Tesla Charging, the company's charging account on X, announced 16 new Supercharger sites with 178 stalls from Monday, Oct 19, through Sunday, Oct 25: nine in the United States with 124 stalls, one in Canada with 12 and six in Europe and Asia with 42. All 16 are listed as open, and every stall is a V4 post, according to the volunteer-run tracker supercharge.info.

The largest are a 24-stall station at Brays Oaks Crossing on South Sam Houston Parkway West in Houston, posted at 2:06 p.m. EDT Friday, and a 24-stall station at a Target on West Hillsboro Boulevard in Deerfield Beach, Florida (post). Both are listed at 325 kW, as are five more North American sites; the six in Europe and Asia are listed at 250 kW (supercharge.info). In Tampa, an eight-stall site at a Wawa on North 30th Street is open around the clock (post).

The 500 kW sites

Three sites are listed at 500 kW: 16 stalls at a Good 2 Go in Camp Verde, Arizona, open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. (post); eight at San Marin Plaza in Novato, California (post); and eight at Harrah's Cherokee Casino Resort in Cherokee, North Carolina, two of them trailer-friendly. Tesla called Cherokee a "Voting Winner Site" (post), chosen through its Supercharger voting page.

Open to other EVs

Four North American sites list both NACS and CCS1 connectors at every stall, so other EVs can plug in without an adapter: Kingston, Ontario; Latham, New York; Waynesburg, Pennsylvania; and Valley View, Texas. The two sites on either side of the Suwon–Munsan Expressway in Goyang, South Korea, list the same pair of plugs. The sites in Bolu, Turkey; Alès, France; and Potenza, Italy, use CCS2 plugs. Nakhon Pathom, Thailand, at a Global House store, is the only one of the 16 listed for Teslas only.

One U.S. site belongs to Supercharger for Business, Tesla's program for stations owned by other businesses: Victron Energy's eight stalls at Texas Best Smokehouse on Interstate 35 in Valley View, Texas (post). The program "grew to over 600 stalls," Tesla said in its third-quarter shareholder update.

Megachargers

Tesla Charging posted no new Megacharger site this week. The shareholder update says Tesla "deployed the first pre-assembled Megacharger to support the Tesla Semi ramp," and its "first Accordion Supercharger, which is pre-assembled in the factory and cheaper to install" (Exhibit 99.1). Tesla counted 8,904 Supercharger stations and 84,465 connectors at the end of September, the update shows.

Quick Hit

Not on the feed. supercharge.info logged 13 more openings with 82 stalls from Oct 19 to Oct 25 that Tesla Charging did not post, among them 16 stalls in Wikieup, Arizona, and eight 500 kW stalls each in Tillamook, Oregon; Bedminster, New Jersey; and Holzkirchen, Germany. Six of the 13 are in China.

V · Products & the Street

The Roadster goes on show in Los Angeles

The Thrust prototype sits in the Petersen Automotive Museum's lobby through Tuesday.

The Petersen Automotive Museum is showing the "2028 Tesla Roadster Thrust Prototype" in its main lobby from Saturday, October 24, through Tuesday, October 27, calling it the "last best driver's car." Tesla lists three trims: Performance, under 1.6 seconds from 0 to 60 mph with a $50,000 reservation; Thrust, under 0.9 seconds with a $250,000 reservation; and the invitation-only Rocket, under 0.59 seconds and not road legal, with production in 2028, EV reported.

"Inspired by fighter jet cockpits, Roadster's interface was designed around the pilot," Tesla (@Tesla) posted at 4:32 p.m. EDT Friday. "55 new Tesla Semi trucks spotted in Nevada," Sawyer Merritt posted at 10:51 p.m. EDT Sunday, adding: "Production seems to be going well!"

The app goes big

"Tesla app now on the big screens," Tesla posted at 9:01 p.m. EDT Friday. The redesigned app for iPad, iPhone Duo and Android tablets and foldables puts the menu on the left and a large vehicle visualization on the right, with a new Show psi button for tire pressures and a 3D home view for Tesla Energy customers, Not a Tesla App reported. Tesla UI engineer Alex Guichet said it "may take an extra week or two for everyone to get it."

Barclays upgrades on a possible SpaceX tie-up

Barclays raised Tesla to Overweight from Equal Weight and lifted its price target to $450 from $370, about 21% above Friday's close, CNBC reported at 6:49 a.m. EDT Monday. "With a combination heavily expected, and with expectation for some premium to TSLA stock, we believe this should drive upside for the stock," analyst Dan Levy wrote; he also cited auto growth, exports from Giga Shanghai, an energy business nearly sold out for 2027 and rising robotaxi miles. Of 49 analysts tracked by LSEG, 25 rate the shares a buy and 22 a hold (CNBC).

Canaccord Genuity's George Gianarikas called last week's earnings call "one of the most unapologetically bullish Tesla calls in many a moon" and reiterated a $442 target, according to his note as posted by Merritt at 12:10 a.m. EDT Monday.

A retail letter to the board

A group of retail shareholders led by Alexandra Merz and Amy Steffens launched a letter on Friday asking Tesla's board and special committee to bring a SpaceX combination to a vote "as soon as the terms are negotiated and documented," TeslaNorth reported. Steffens's tally on Saturday evening was 6,493 signers holding 17,528,859 shares, figures that are self-reported and not independently verified; Merz said it would go to the board on Monday. "Tesla needs chips and capital. SpaceX needs Megapacks for Colossus," the letter says. Neither company has commented, and the December 3 annual meeting has no merger item on the ballot (TeslaNorth).
