The Morning Picture

SpaceX and Tesla merger talks gather pace

Boards and advisers at SpaceX and Tesla are working through the terms and governance of a combination that could be announced as soon as December, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, according to Drive Tesla. A deal would join two companies worth more than $3.4 trillion together, and the people cited said the announcement could also slip into early 2027. TSLA rose 1.7% to $397.45, touching its high for the day after the report.

By Friday morning, Texas's incoming attorney general, Nathan Johnson, had said any such merger "warrants tremendous scrutiny," Quartz reported. Closer to the showroom, Tesla took its $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive off the US order page, and a European software update quietly capped how far FSD may drive over the speed limit.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Europe's FSD update caps speed at 10% over the limit

Update 2026.39.100 does more than rename the system, owners found; referrals reach Canada and Mexico, and Robotaxi passes five million autonomous miles.

Tesla's European update 2026.39.100, which carries FSD v14.2.2.6, renames FSD (Supervised) to Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD). Its release notes say the change "only updates the name of certain features and text in your vehicle, and does not change the way your features behave," but owners found that it also lowers the maximum Speed Offset from +50% to +10% and resets it to 0% at the start of every drive, Not a Tesla App reported on Thursday afternoon. Speed Offset lets the system drive above the posted limit by a set percentage.

The two changes match what the Dutch vehicle authority, the RDW, approved earlier this month. France, Sweden and the European Transport Safety Council had raised concerns about the system driving well over the limit, and Germany said it would back FSD in the coming vote on EU-wide approval if Tesla capped Speed Offset at 10% and adopted the new name. Both are now live ahead of the December vote.

Quick Hit

Zoox moves downtown. Amazon's Zoox opened robotaxi rides to downtown Las Vegas and the Arts District on Thursday, rolled out a redesigned vehicle and extended service to 6 a.m. to 3 a.m. daily, the Las Vegas Review-Journal reported.

FSD referrals reach Canada and Mexico

Owners in Canada and Mexico can now give other Tesla owners a free month of FSD (Supervised), Drive Tesla reported on Thursday evening; the program started in the United States on Oct 29. If the recipient subscribes, the referrer gets a free month or, if they bought FSD outright, $99 in Tesla Credits, up to 12 rewards a year. Vehicle referrals have been removed in Canada.

Robotaxi passes five million autonomous miles

Tesla's Robotaxi page showed 5,047,894 autonomous miles on Nov 12, the day after the service went to 24-hour operation in all eight of its cities, Self Drive News reported. Tesla's Robotaxi Safety Report had put the total at 3.1 million miles as of Oct 18. Tesla has not said how many vehicles run overnight.

II · Sales & Demand

The $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive leaves the order page

The cheapest new all-wheel-drive Model Y now costs $8,000 more, Premium orders are quoted for 2027, and the Wall Connector goes up again.

Tesla's US configurator no longer lists the $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive, leaving the $49,990 Model Y Premium All-Wheel Drive as the cheapest new all-wheel-drive version buyers can order, TeslaNorth reported. Tesla gave no explanation. The trim can still be bought from stock: Tesla's inventory page listed units at $42,990 and $43,990 on Friday morning, each rated at 294 miles, EV (eletric-vehicles.com) reported. Tesla added the trim on Feb 2, and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) rated a 2027 model-year version at 294 miles in a record dated Nov 3.

The Model Y line now has five versions: Rear-Wheel Drive at $39,990, Premium Rear-Wheel Drive at $45,990, Premium All-Wheel Drive at $49,990, Performance at $57,990 and the six-seat Model Y L Premium at $61,990. In TeslaNorth's Seattle checks, both Premium versions showed estimated delivery of January to March 2027 and the Model Y L February to April 2027.

Wall Connector price rises a fourth time

The standard Wall Connector now costs $635 in the US, up from $585, and the Universal Wall Connector $720, up from $660, Not a Tesla App reported on Thursday. It is the fourth increase this year for the standard unit, which started 2026 at $450, a $185 or about 41% rise; Tesla has not given a reason. In Canada the Wall Connector is now $885 CAD and the Universal $1,015 CAD, both up $70, Drive Tesla reported.

III · Energy & Charging

SpaceX plans 24 GWh of batteries in the Arizona desert

The $50 billion supercompute campus would carry the largest battery system in the US, though no supplier has been named; five new Superchargers since Thursday morning.

SpaceX on Thursday put a $50 billion price on a supercompute campus in Cochise County, Arizona, with nine buildings, up to 6 gigawatts (GW) of power and 24 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of battery storage, alongside a separate $24 billion facility at Starbase, Texas, TeslaNorth reported. SpaceX's project page calls the battery system the largest in the United States. The company targets construction in 2027, and a county zoning hearing is set for Dec 9.

The announcement does not name Tesla as the battery supplier or confirm a Megapack order, TeslaNorth noted. SpaceX is already a Megapack customer: it bought $242 million of Megapacks in the second quarter, bringing its 2026 purchases to $329 million after $87 million in the first quarter, for xAI data center sites, EE Power reported.

Five new Superchargers

Since Thursday morning the Tesla Charging account has announced a 24-stall Supercharger on North Rancho Drive in North Las Vegas, Nevada, and a 12-stall site in Silver Spring, Maryland, plus eight-stall sites in Palm City, Florida, The Dalles, Oregon, and Bellingham, Washington. Tesla called Bellingham a voting-winner site, picked by owners through its site-suggestion vote.

IV · Corporate & Governance

Merger talks with SpaceX are said to progress

Terms, governance and China are on the table; Texas's incoming attorney general promises a hard look.

The boards and advisers of SpaceX and Tesla are working through the terms of a possible combination, including how the combined company would be governed and how many SpaceX shares Tesla shareholders would receive, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, according to Drive Tesla. The companies are exploring ways to ring-fence Tesla's operations in China because of SpaceX's US government and defense contracts. No agreement has been announced, and neither company commented.

SpaceX, public since June, is valued at more than $1.9 trillion and Tesla at about $1.5 trillion. Tesla's annual meeting is on Dec 3, Quartz noted, citing Bloomberg. On SpaceX's October earnings call Musk said special committees of independent directors were studying a deal: "Because I'm a conflicted party, I can't actually be in the discussions of the special committees." He said a merger would be a good idea but needs the backing of 50% of all Tesla shareholders, not just a majority of votes cast.

Texas's next attorney general promises scrutiny

"Any merger of companies of that consequence warrants tremendous scrutiny," Nathan Johnson, the Democrat elected Texas attorney general on Nov 3, told Bloomberg on Thursday, Quartz reported. He would not discuss details of a deal but said, "I have no qualms about examining his relationships to the state." Johnson, who takes office in January, said neither he nor his transition team has contacted Musk: "If Elon wants to talk, we can talk."

V · SEC Filings

No new Tesla filings since Thursday morning

Big holders' quarterly reports show Deutsche Bank adding and JPMorgan and Fidelity trimming.

No filings for Tesla, Inc. (CIK 0001318605) were accepted on the SEC's Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval system (EDGAR) between 8:00 a.m. EST Thursday and 8:43 a.m. EST Friday.

What the big holders reported

In third-quarter 13F reports, Deutsche Bank raised its Tesla holding 8.5% to 10.42 million shares, its most since 2019 (EV); JPMorgan cut 21.1% to 33.11 million (EV); and Fidelity's parent, FMR, trimmed 2.4% to 38.82 million (EV).
