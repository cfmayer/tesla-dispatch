The Morning Picture

The Cybercab reaches Dallas, and Italy says yes to FSD

Tesla opened public rides in the Cybercab, its two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals, in Dallas on Thursday, the second city after Austin. "Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city," the company's Robotaxi account posted at 11:47 a.m. EST, and riders can book through the Tesla Robotaxi app, Teslarati reported. Tesla's Robotaxi lead called the Dallas cars a new "V1.5" version without saying what had changed, EV (eletric-vehicles.com) noted.

On Friday morning Tesla said its supervised driver-assistance software, sold in Europe as Tesla Assisted Driving, is "now approved in Italy," the 12th EU country to allow it. Tesla also opened orders in Argentina, took its Supercharger Waitlist to 300 US sites, and filed new proxy material ahead of the Dec 3 annual meeting. TSLA fell 2.4% to $374.41 on Thursday as all three major US indexes dropped about 2%.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Cybercab rides open in a second city

Dallas riders spot a trunk camera on the "V1.5" cars; Musk names Florida and California as later steps, and Italy approves the supervised system.

Tesla made the Dallas launch official with a video post on Thursday and later reposted footage of the cars on city streets with the caption "Cybercabs in Big D," EV reported. Robotaxi customers received an email titled "Arriving Now in Dallas." Tesla's main account quoted the launch at 3:02 p.m. EST with "Cybercab expands to second city," and Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's AI software chief, added at 4:39 p.m. EST: "And at almost no extra effort on the engineering side."

Tesla has run unsupervised Model Y robotaxis in Dallas since April, and the Cybercab joins the same network, Teslarati said. Public Cybercab rides began in limited areas of Austin in early September. Tesla's Cybercab questions-and-answers page now lists rides in limited areas of Austin and Dallas, TeslaNorth found. Invited customers can buy the car for personal use at $29,990 plus a required $299 monthly autonomy subscription, with deliveries targeted for December.

A camera in the trunk lid

Eric Earley, whose X profile describes him as Tesla's "Robotaxi Leader (Engineering, Operations, Content and Growth)," wrote "Cybercab V1.5 in Dallas now!" Riders went looking for the changes: a Dallas owner posted a nine-second clip showing a camera in the rear trunk lid, presumably so the car can check whether a passenger left luggage or groceries behind, TeslaNorth reported. Tesla has not said whether the camera is new or what it is for, and the rear-hatch Starlink panel on the Dallas cars predates the V1.5 label, EV noted.

An $11 first ride

Barrett Linburg, who said on X that he took "one of the first CyberCab rides in Dallas right after they went live," described a 20-minute loop from near Southern Methodist University to the Knox district and back; the car arrived in two minutes and the trip cost $11, EV reported. "Driving was flawless even through a construction zone although it avoided getting on Central Expressway," he wrote. The Robotaxi account quoted his post at 5:33 p.m. EST: "Come try it out." Cybercabs appear in the Robotaxi app alongside the Model Y; Tesla has not published the Dallas Cybercab service area or the number of cars in service there.

Texas, then Florida, then perhaps California

"We are being very careful with the rollout of Cybercab because it has no steering wheel and pedals, so there's no fallback," Musk told Gavin Baker, managing partner and chief investment officer of Atreides Management, at the TomorrowX Summit, speaking by video from Shenzhen, according to a transcript published Thursday. "Thus far we've had no serious injuries or accidents with rollout of Robotaxi," he said. Tesla is "expanding to large numbers of Cybercabs in other major Texas cities, and then Florida," and added: "I think perhaps early next year we'll get approval to operate in California."

Quick Hit

Waymo maps Tampa's airport. Waymo said it is testing autonomously with a specialist at the wheel at Tampa International Airport, while its driverless freeway rides stay paused nationwide after a California order on Nov 11, WTSP reported.

Italy approves Tesla Assisted Driving

"Mamma, butta la pasta. Now approved in Italy. Rollout begins soon," Tesla Europe, Middle East & Africa posted at 5:21 a.m. EST Friday, using the Italian phrase for "Mom, put the pasta on." Italy is the first of the EU's four most populous countries to allow the system ahead of a bloc-wide vote, and the 12th member state counting the Netherlands, whose vehicle authority RDW granted the first approval on April 10, EV reported. "After a thorough technical feasibility check, the ministry has given the green light to the provisional type approval" of the system, "anticipating the process of recognition at EU level," Transport Minister Matteo Salvini wrote on X, Reuters reported (translated from Italian). Italy's roughly 58.9 million people lift the share of EU residents in countries that allow the system to about 29%, from about 16%.

Tesla did not say which cars qualify or when the update arrives. It will cost 99 euros a month, matching the Dutch subscription, according to Quattroruote, which noted that Salvini and Musk spoke by phone about the system in recent weeks. The approval follows Finland on Nov 10.

Three weeks after a call with Salvini

In April, Italy's Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport told a Tesla owner it would take no decision until EU-level talks concluded. After Musk spoke with Salvini, who is also deputy prime minister, on Oct 29, Salvini wrote that Italy "supports a swift EU approval of Tesla Assisted Driving," EV recalled. Approval across the bloc needs a qualified majority in the European Commission's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles: at least 15 of the 27 member states, representing at least 65% of the EU population. Germany's Transport Ministry has said a vote is planned for December. Germany, France and Spain, the other three of the four most populous members, have not recognized the Dutch approval, and national recognition does not bind a country's vote.

II · Sales & Demand

Tesla opens orders in Argentina

The Model 3 starts at $40,990 and the Model Y at $43,990; China's car market keeps shrinking.

Tesla's online configurator for Argentina went live on Thursday night, ahead of the Nov 20 launch date the company had announced, making Argentina its fourth market in South America after Chile, Colombia and Uruguay, TeslaNorth reported. Prices are shown in US dollars alongside peso figures: the Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive costs $40,990, the Model 3 Premium Long Range Rear-Wheel Drive $46,490 and the Model 3 Performance All-Wheel Drive $53,990; the Model Y Premium Rear-Wheel Drive costs $43,990 and the Model Y Premium Long Range Rear-Wheel Drive $49,990.

Reservation and destination fees bring the base Model 3 to $41,590 and the base Model Y to $44,590. The configurator lists a $119 monthly subscription under Full Self-Driving Capability, billed from delivery, which is not an announcement that FSD (Supervised) will run on Argentine roads. Shopping-center operator Cencosud has said Tesla's first Argentine showroom will open at Unicenter near Buenos Aires in late November.

China's market slides further

Passenger-car retail sales in China fell 25% from a year earlier to 611,000 in the first 15 days of November, and retail sales of new energy vehicles (NEVs) fell 13% to 447,000, according to China Passenger Car Association data reported by CnEVPost. UBS analyst Patrick Hummel, keeping a Neutral rating and a $425 target on Tesla, wrote on Thursday that wholesale volumes fell almost as much as retail, that he saw no sign of improvement even against a lower base, and that the Chinese market remains a challenge that tends to weigh on the sector's shares, dpa-AFX reported.

III · Energy & Charging

Supercharger Waitlist reaches 300 US sites

A virtual queue for busy stations goes national a week before Thanksgiving; Powerwall 3P goes on sale in Switzerland.

"Waitlist is now live at 300 Supercharger sites across the US," Tesla Charging posted at 5:29 p.m. EST Thursday, adding that drivers of other brands can join through the Tesla app. The rollout is a big jump from a five-site pilot that began in May in California and New York, TeslaNorth reported. At a busy site, drivers tap Join Waitlist in the app or on the touchscreen, and a Tesla navigating to the site can join automatically as it gets close. When their turn comes, drivers have three minutes to plug in, with an option to add 30 seconds; no stall is assigned, and the queue runs on the honor system. It needs app version 4.56.5 or vehicle software 2026.14 or later.

Five new Supercharger sites

Tesla Charging announced new sites in Benton, Arkansas (16 stalls), Welshpool, UK (12 stalls), Des Peres, Missouri (8 stalls), Date, Japan (4 stalls) and Stykkishólmur, Iceland (4 stalls) between Thursday afternoon and early Friday.

Powerwall 3P on sale in Switzerland

"Three-phase Powerwall 3P is now available to order in Switzerland," Tesla Energy posted at 11:04 a.m. EST Thursday. The battery combines storage and an inverter for homes with three-phase connections and follows a launch in Portugal earlier this year, TeslaNorth reported. Swiss buyers submit their details and a Tesla-certified installer quotes the job; the system needs a Gateway, carries a 10-year warranty, includes Tesla Home energy management and cannot be combined with Powerwall 2 or standard Powerwall 3 units.

IV · Optimus, AI & the Roadster

Musk: Optimus could do the dishes in 12 to 18 months

An "authentic" robot demo may come within a month or two; Tesla's car computer plays Diablo, and the Roadster goes on show in Los Angeles.

Asked by Baker when a robot could take out the trash and put away the dishes at home, Musk said: "I think Optimus will be capable of doing the things you described in about 12 to 18 months," according to the TomorrowX transcript. Taking a dirty plate, wiping it, washing it, drying it and putting it away "is actually very— there's no humanoid robot that can do that," he said.

Musk said Tesla will show Optimus "when we can do a demo that we feel that the demo is authentic and not just staged or pre-programmed," adding that "it might be, I don't know, in a month or two." The first use, he said, would "probably" be heavy industry, such as mining and refining ore, and jobs that run 168 hours a week, before homes: "I think pretty much everyone's going to want one, if not two."

Tesla's AI plays Diablo

"Tesla's AI, running on our little AI4 computer, can now reach Torment II in Diablo"

Elon Musk (@elonmusk), 5:34 a.m. EST Friday

AI4 is the computer in current Tesla cars, the hardware that runs FSD (Supervised) v14.3.13. Musk did not say which game in the series, which model or how the run was set up.

The Roadster at the LA Auto Show

The LA Auto Show, which opens on Friday and runs through Nov 29 at the Los Angeles Convention Center, posted photos of a covered Roadster and teased a "cover pull," MotorBiscuit reported. Tesla unveiled the production car on Oct 15 at SpaceX's test site near Waco, Texas, and claims about 1 megawatt (1,340 hp) from its two rear motors; vehicle engineering chief Lars Moravy cited a top speed above 230 mph. Tesla reportedly plans three versions: a road-ready Performance model claimed to reach 60 mph in under 1.6 seconds, a race-ready Thrust version with rear thrusters (under 0.9 seconds) and an invitation-only, single-seat Rocket edition with ten thrusters (under 0.59 seconds).

V · SEC Filings

A new video for the shareholder vote

Tesla filed its "impossible possible" video and a VoteTesla.com update, two weeks before the Dec 3 annual meeting.

Tesla filed additional proxy materials (DEFA14A) at 5:15 p.m. EST Thursday containing a post it made on X that day, with a transcript of the video, and an updated VoteTesla.com site, according to the filing. "We're building a world transported by electric vehicles, powered by clean energy and supported by autonomous robots," the video says. "Our progress has not been guaranteed, but we've continued to find ways to make the impossible possible. And the result is an age of amazing abundance where anyone can have anything they can think of." Musk closes it: "It's a future where our civilization exists beyond the stars where all that we have envisioned becomes real."

"Vote with Tesla on all proposals ahead of our Annual Meeting on December 3," Tesla posted with the video at 10:30 a.m. EST. Most holders must vote by 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 2, according to an earlier filing. The filing names directors Elon Musk, Robyn Denholm, Ira Ehrenpreis, Joe Gebbia, Jack Hartung, James Murdoch, Kimbal Musk, JB Straubel and Kathleen Wilson-Thompson, plus executive officers Vaibhav Taneja, the chief financial officer, and Tom Zhu, as participants in the solicitation.
