The Morning Picture

Robotaxi goes around the clock as the Cybertruck gets a summer reprieve

Tesla's Robotaxi service now runs 24 hours a day in all eight cities where it offers rides, the company's Robotaxi account posted late Wednesday, ending the overnight pause weeks ahead of the year-end goal Tesla set on its October earnings call. "Robotaxi works 168 hours a week," Elon Musk (@elonmusk) added at 12:12 a.m. EST Thursday.

Earlier Wednesday, Musk said Cybertruck production will run "through the end of summer 2027" to work through more of the order backlog, a few months past the mid-2027 end date he gave last week, CBS News reported. The first 50 Semis for Chicago are on their way, and TSLA rose 2.9% to $390.84.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Robotaxi drops its overnight shutdown

Round-the-clock rides in five Texas and three Florida cities; Waymo edges back ahead on the Texas register, and a report says Musk made the call to drop Basic Autopilot.

"Robotaxi now runs 24/7," the official @robotaxi account posted at 11:31 p.m. EST Wednesday. "Every city we're available in." The service covers Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio in Texas and Miami, Orlando and Tampa in Florida, with a fleet of Model Ys and Cybercabs, EV (eletric-vehicles.com) reported. Fort Worth was added on Nov 2 and San Antonio on Nov 9; Las Vegas, Phoenix and Jacksonville are listed as coming soon, without dates.

Tesla has lengthened the day in steps: 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. in late August, to 11 p.m. in October, to midnight by the Oct 21 quarterly update and to 2 a.m. at the end of October. On the Oct 21 earnings call, Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's vice president of AI software, said hours were "driven by the capability of the AI software" and that "over the rest of this year we'll unlock 24/7 operation, go to higher-speed roads." He added: "We just want to make sure that there's not a single safety incident. That is our operating principle." The announcement adds no new highway access, service areas or cities, TeslaNorth noted. Waymo, which competes in six of the eight cities directly and in Austin through Uber, already runs around the clock.

Waymo edges back ahead on the Texas register

A day after Tesla first passed Waymo on the Texas automated-vehicle register, the count on Thursday stood at 1,303 for Tesla (573 Cybercabs and 730 Model Ys) and 1,305 for Waymo, according to the Robotaxi Tracker site, which reads the state's records, EV reported. Austin remains by far the largest Tesla operation, with more than 400 autonomous vehicles registered there; registrations run ahead of the cars riders see in service.

Report: Musk ordered the end of Basic Autopilot

Musk personally decided to stop including Basic Autopilot (Traffic-Aware Cruise Control plus Autosteer) on new cars to lift FSD subscriptions, Not a Tesla App reported on Wednesday, citing a source with knowledge of the decision. Since January, new US and Canadian cars have come with Traffic-Aware Cruise Control only, and lane-centering requires FSD, sold in North America only as a monthly subscription since February. One milestone of Musk's 2025 pay award requires 10 million active FSD subscriptions; Tesla reported a record 1.74 million in the third quarter, and FSD was included with 75% of new North American orders.

Quick Hit

Cybercab in Korea. Tesla Korea's Cybercab display at Starfield Anseong opens today and runs through Nov 15, the car's first showing in the country; it is not for sale there, Car-ticle reported.

II · Sales & Demand

The Cybertruck line runs to the end of summer 2027

Musk adds a few months to clear more of the backlog, calling it sentiment rather than profit; Chicago's first 50 Semis are on the road.

"In order to work through more of the Cybertruck order backlog, production will be extended through the end of summer 2027," Musk posted at 12:11 p.m. EST Wednesday. Twenty minutes later he added: "This is not the smartest move from a profit-maximizing standpoint btw, but there must be some room in this world for sentimentality or what's the point?" On Nov 6 he had said Giga Texas would wind the truck down "around the middle of next year" to make room for Cybercab, and on Nov 9 that the line would shut "in about 8 months," Electrek reported.

The announcement does not promise that every order will be filled or say which buyers may still get refunds, Not a Tesla App noted. Tesla paused Dual Motor AWD orders on Nov 6 and closed Premium AWD ordering by Nov 10, leaving the Cyberbeast, now $109,990 after two increases in less than a week, as the only trim on sale. Some long-time supporters pushed back on the wind-down itself; FSD tester Chuck Cook wrote "Cybercab won't haul my bees," Electrek reported.

Chicago's first 50 Semis are en route

"Hello Windy City," the Tesla Semi account posted at noon EST Wednesday. "Chicago's first 50 Semis en route for delivery." Tesla did not name the customer, but the only 50-truck order tied to the city belongs to HMD Trucking, a family-owned Chicago carrier with more than 650 mostly diesel trucks, Electrek reported; owner Henry Malukas has put the deal at about $15 million. HMD took its first Semi on Nov 2, the first registered in Illinois, after averaging 1.74 kilowatt-hours (kWh) per mile over more than 4,000 test miles, and will start with local runs around Chicago, Drive Tesla reported. "Tesla has been transparent with us regarding the capabilities of Semi every step of the way and it has not disappointed," said Chief Operating Officer Michael Mahler.

III · Energy & Charging

Testing a battery site before it meets the grid

Tesla explains the simulation rig behind Melbourne's 444-Megapack hub; four new Superchargers, a Megacharger corridor west of Chicago and a Semi service center in Laredo.

Tesla's Controller-Hardware-In-the-Loop (CHIL) setup lets it "simulate the entire site in real time before it's connected to the grid" and see how it responds "to faults, frequency changes or if a tree were to fall on a power line," the Tesla Megapack account posted on Nov 10. It said CHIL validated 444 grid-forming Megapacks at the Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub before connection, "delivering 600 MW / 1.6 GWh of storage months ahead of schedule." Musk quoted it at 1:49 p.m. EST Wednesday: "Tesla Megapacks are designed to ensure grid stability, so that you never have a blackout."

The schedule claim is Tesla's own, without a comparison to the original plan; Victoria's government declared the hub fully operational on Dec 6, 2025, TeslaNorth reported. The separate Big Canberra Battery at Williamsdale, 136 Megapack 2 XL units for 250 megawatts (MW) and 500 megawatt-hours (MWh), went online on Nov 5.

Four new Superchargers

Since Wednesday morning the Tesla Charging account has announced a 24-stall Supercharger on West Skye Canyon Park Drive in Las Vegas, plus eight-stall sites in New Castle, Delaware, Wandering River, Alberta, and Mission, Texas.

Megachargers line up west of Chicago

Tuesday's six-stall Hodgkins, Illinois, Megacharger is one of four Semi charging sites that appeared on Tesla's map this week, alongside Urbandale, Iowa (seven stalls), Ogallala, Nebraska (two) and Riverdale, Utah (two, at up to 414 kilowatts), EVwire reported. Together they form a corridor heading west from Chicago and nearly double Tesla's public Megacharger network to nine locations; the nearest open site to Hodgkins is Urbandale, about 330 miles west.

Laredo Semi service center opens today

Tesla holds an invite-only opening for its Semi Service Center at 13505 Regional Drive in Laredo, Texas, today, with site tours and Semi ride-alongs, Hoodline reported. The listing gives no bay count or regular service hours. Texas licensing records show a $1 million, 29,805-square-foot renovation for an electric-vehicle repair and service center at the address. Tesla was hiring Semi service managers, supervisors and instructors in 11 US cities, including Laredo, as of mid-October. The service push follows the start of volume production on Sep 24 at the Semi factory in Sparks, Nevada, which Tesla says is built for 50,000 trucks a year; Tesla does not report Semi output or deliveries, Electrek noted.
