The Morning Picture

TSLA jumps 11.5% as FSD v15 clips spread and merger talks take shape

Tesla shares rose 11.46% to $385.46 on Monday as the FSD (Supervised) v15 rollout and talk of a SpaceX combination met a market rebound sparked by Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang; the Nasdaq 100 gained 6.86%. Tesla spent the afternoon sharing owners' first v15 drives, from two hours in Manhattan to a car steering around a fallen branch, while the software quietly dropped the menu that asked drivers why they took over.

The Financial Times reported that five directors, three from Tesla and two from SpaceX, now lead the committees negotiating an all-stock merger, with Evercore advising Tesla. Tesla filed new letters urging shareholders to vote before the Dec. 3 annual meeting, and its energy and charging accounts promoted Powerwall's Storm Watch mode and new categories for reporting broken chargers.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Owners' first v15 drives, shared by Tesla

Tesla reposted a thread of early clips, the takeover menu is gone, and engineers describe how little of the AI4 computer is left.

"First v15 experiences & scenarios from our owners in thread below," Tesla posted at 3:58 p.m. EST Monday, followed by a string of owner clips. One shows a car steering around "a huge branch blocking the road"; others show deer avoidance and a new parking sequence, pedestrian rendering, the car seeing "chain link fence and power lines," and parking in an owner's own spot. In the longest clip two owners drove almost two hours through Manhattan; one wrote that "for almost TWO HOURS, the car didn't make a single mistake." At 5:24 p.m. EST Tesla added a pothole-avoidance clip: "v15 keeps your wheels & tires safe." Ashok Elluswamy, who leads Tesla's AI software, wrote at 2:37 a.m. EST Tuesday of another owner's video: "Unreal rendering without Unreal engine."

No more "why did you take over" menu

With v15.0.1 in software update 2026.38.6.11, drivers who take control are no longer asked to pick a reason, The Electric Viking reported on Tuesday; they can still report problems with voice notes. The menu arrived with v14.3.2 in April and asked drivers to choose Navigation, Parking, Critical or Other, with no close button. Tesla's release notes do not mention the change. Installation data cited by the site show 11 installs, all on Nov. 22 and all on US Model Y cars with AI4, where FSD handled an average of 96.4% of each of 27 drives.

What Reality View shows, and what it does not

Reality View branches off the same vision-encoder data the driving model uses and runs it through a separate model of about one billion parameters, on a small slice of the car's computer, Not a Tesla App explained on Monday. The site cautioned that the new view is closer to camera video mapped onto 3D shapes than a direct readout of detections: an object drawn in 3D has likely been detected, but flat things such as crosswalks or traffic lights may simply be part of the image. A Model 3 owner who goes by vogel rebuilt a similar view from 13 minutes of his car's six-camera footage and found the cameras up to 8.7 milliseconds out of sync with each other; he has published the project as open source.

"The last few percents" of AI4

Dilreet Raju, who works on Tesla's AI silicon, wrote that the hardest work of his life was co-designing the v15 vision encoder "to squeeze out the last few percents of MFU from AI4," Electrek reported; MFU, model FLOPs utilization, measures how much of a chip's theoretical compute a model actually uses. Musk's description of Reality View running on the AI4 board's second chip, SoC B, raises a question Tesla has not answered: how much of AI4 it reserves for failover if one chip fails without a driver ready to take over, the site noted.

Quick Hit

Regulators. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is investigating roughly 2.9 million FSD-equipped vehicles over reports of cars running red lights, crossing railroad tracks improperly and driving into oncoming lanes, The Verge noted in its v15 review.

II · Energy & Charging

Powerwall tops up before storms

Tesla Energy promoted Storm Watch, and the app gained more ways to flag a broken charger.

"Ahead of extreme weather, Storm Watch mode automatically charges your Powerwall to 100%," Tesla Energy posted at 6:41 p.m. EST Monday with a short video.

More ways to report a broken charger

"Spotted an issue at a Supercharger, Megacharger, or Destination Charger? Use Report an Issue in the Tesla app, now with additional categories," Tesla Charging posted at 7:14 p.m. EST. "Reports go to our 24/7 Operations team, helping us fix them faster."

III · Robotaxi

Robotaxi app code points to scheduled rides

Version 26.11.5 shipped Monday as "minor fixes," but its code describes booking ahead, longer waits and lock-screen trip updates.

Tesla released version 26.11.5 of its Robotaxi app on Monday with App Store release notes that say only "Minor fixes and improvements," but code in the update shows Tesla is building rides that can be booked in advance, Drive Tesla reported on Tuesday, citing a breakdown by the account Tesla App Updates. Riders would enter a pickup point, destination and time; they could change the pickup window until a cutoff, and the app would check whether a car is free at that time and suggest alternatives if not. Tesla has not said when scheduled rides will launch.

A little longer at the curb

Riders can also ask a car that arrives early to wait longer, and the app shows how long it will wait. That feature already appears to be live: one rider, Ethan McKanna, wrote that he extended his wait "from 7 to 15 minutes" in two-minute steps, at no charge. The update also adds lock-screen notifications for ride confirmation, a reminder to fasten seatbelts, and when the car is pulling over or reaching the destination.

IV · The SpaceX Talks

Three Tesla directors at the merger table

Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung represent Tesla on the committees weighing an all-stock combination, the Financial Times reported.

Tesla and SpaceX have named five directors to the special committees negotiating a possible all-stock merger: Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung from Tesla, and Roelof Botha and Randy Glein from SpaceX, the Financial Times reported on Tuesday, according to Investing.com. Evercore advises Tesla's committee; Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs advise SpaceX's. The advisers are building the financial models behind each side's position on an exchange ratio, and the talks are working toward a possible year-end deadline, though people involved said the committees were not yet close to agreement.

Musk is barred from formally taking part in the negotiations under Texas governance rules, though the committees can consult senior executives. Tesla would need a separate shareholder meeting, and the deal would need more than 50% of all possible votes, with abstentions and uncast votes counting against it. Musk holds about 84% of the voting rights at SpaceX but around 20% at Tesla, the report said. A combination could face reviews in the United States, China, Europe and the United Kingdom, and Bloomberg reported earlier this month that a deal could be announced as early as December, Reuters noted.

V · SEC Filings

"Time Is Running Out": letters to shareholders

Tesla filed two shareholder letters, an updated VoteTesla.com and digital ads, nine days before the annual meeting.

Tesla filed additional proxy materials (DEFA14A) at 5:15 p.m. EST Monday, according to the filing: two letters it sent shareholders that day, a copy of its updated VoteTesla.com site and digital and social advertisements. "At this time of year, we reflect on what we are thankful for — and that includes you, our shareholders," the first letter from the board begins. It asks holders to vote for the re-election of the three Class I directors, Elon Musk, Robyn Denholm and JB Straubel, and for "ALL proposals on the ballot." The second, headed "Time Is Running Out," says "Tesla's next chapter starts with your vote." For most shareholders the deadline is 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec. 2; the meeting is Dec. 3.
