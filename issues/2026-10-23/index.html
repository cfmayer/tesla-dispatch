The Morning Picture

A special committee, in writing

"The Tesla board of directors has formed a special committee (the 'Special Committee') comprising disinterested directors to explore deepening Tesla's relationship with SpaceX, which could be through certain arrangements including without limitation a business combination," Tesla says in its third-quarter 10-Q, filed late Wednesday. "While there could be merits to a Transaction, there is no assurance that any such Transaction will be consummated," it adds; any deal would need the committee's recommendation and any required shareholder approval. SpaceX's own 10-Q, filed at 4:03 p.m. EDT Thursday, says its board has formed a committee too and that "no determination" has been made.

The shares fell 3.08% to $374.58 on Thursday after Tesla cut its 2026 capital spending forecast to about $20 billion from more than $25 billion, "raising concerns among investors counting on heavy investment for Elon Musk's AI and robotics vision," Reuters reported. "The risk is Tesla now enters a period of limbo while investors await news on a potential combination with SpaceX," AJ Bell's Russ Mould told the agency. At least nine brokers raised their price targets on Thursday, Yahoo Finance data show.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Robotaxi stretches to 1 a.m. in Austin

The service area now reaches the Formula 1 circuit ahead of race weekend, and later hours are coming.

"Tesla has just expanded its Robotaxi service area in Austin, Texas," Sawyer Merritt posted at 1:59 a.m. EDT Friday, adding that the new area "includes the area around Austin's F1 racetrack, where 400,000 people will attend the race festivities this weekend," and that the service "now runs until 1 AM (from 12 AM before)." Ten minutes later he added that service "could be blocked to the actual F1 event, although attendees should still be able to Robotaxi in the surrounding areas."

On Wednesday's call, AI software head Ashok Elluswamy traced the hours: "we had initially had the Robotaxi service from 6:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., then we expanded to 10:00 p.m., 11:00 p.m. Now it's going to be pretty soon 2:00 a.m.," TeslaNorth reported. Asked whether regulators or software limit growth, he said: "It is technology-driven, but we do see that pretty quickly become regulatory, perhaps in Q1, Q2 timeframes."

Next on the map

Tesla's new Robotaxi safety report lists six operating cities, Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando and Tampa, and names Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix and San Antonio as coming next. It counts 862,000 miles driven after dark through October 18, out of 3.1 million autonomous miles in total, TeslaNorth noted.

II · Sales & Demand

No Cybertruck for Australia

Tesla cancelled reservations in Australia and New Zealand and raised U.S. Model Y loan rates.

"After careful consideration, plans to produce Cybertruck for Australia and New Zealand have been discontinued," Tesla Australia told reservation holders on Friday local time, Drive reported. "As a result, we're unable to fulfil your reservation and it will be cancelled. You'll receive a full refund for any payments made." Deposits were A$150, refundable, and had been taken since the truck's 2019 reveal.

As a "thank you for your early support," holders who buy a new Model 3 or Model Y get one year of FSD (Supervised), valued at A$1,788, Drive said; the offer runs through the end of 2026, according to a post by Sawyer Merritt at 7:18 p.m. EDT Thursday. Just 20,237 Cybertrucks were sold in the U.S. last year, down from 38,965 in 2024, Drive said, citing Cox Automotive.

Quick Hit

Costlier loans. Tesla raised U.S. financing rates on base and Premium Model Y trims to 2.49% APR from 1.99%, with the Performance trim unchanged at 3.99%, Sawyer Merritt posted at 12:19 a.m. EDT Friday.

III · Energy & Charging

Fort Bend says yes to "Project Crystal Sun"

County commissioners approved incentives for a proposed $10.1 billion solar plant; the site is not final.

Fort Bend County commissioners approved a tax abatement, a grant agreement, foreign-trade-zone participation and a "True Up" payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreement for Tesla's proposed solar cell manufacturing plant on Thursday, each in a 4–0 vote, with interim County Judge Daniel Wong abstaining "due to his company's work with Tesla," Community Impact reported. The $10.1 billion project would use part of a 3,057-acre site at FM 1994 and FM 762, create about 9,700 direct jobs and start operating in the first quarter of 2029 if the site is chosen; Tesla is weighing another location that has not been made public.

"This is a win for our residents and community partners," Commissioner Dexter McCoy said. A state school-tax limitation under the JETI Act awaits Gov. Greg Abbott's approval (Community Impact; KPRC).

Megapacks and Superchargers

"We are increasing production of Megapacks as much as possible," Musk replied at 2:13 p.m. EDT Thursday to a post quoting his call remark that Tesla is "almost sold out of all Megapacks for 2027." Tesla Charging announced a 16-stall Supercharger in Camp Verde, Arizona (post, 1:24 p.m. EDT).

IV · The SpaceX Question

Two committees, no determination

Both companies' quarterly reports now describe the review of a possible combination.

Tesla's committee is made up of "disinterested directors," and a transaction "would be subject to, among other things, the affirmative recommendation of the Special Committee and, if agreed upon, any necessary shareholder approval," the 10-Q says. SpaceX's 10-Q says its board formed a committee "to evaluate a potential combination between SpaceX and Tesla," that "the board of directors of Tesla has also formed a special committee," and that any deal would need further approval from its committee, its board and "our controlling shareholder."

Reuters said a combination could create a company worth about $3.5 trillion. Capex is a delicate balance in this market, eToro's Josh Gilbert said. "For a stock priced on Cybercab and Optimus, that (capex forecast cut) explains why the shares went nowhere on a result that beat on most measures" (Reuters).

Quick Hit

188 times earnings. Before Thursday's open, Tesla traded at 188.24 times forward earnings estimates, against 6.58 for Ford and 5.16 for General Motors, Reuters noted Thursday morning. At Thursday's close the shares were about 25% below their record intraday high of $498.83, set in December.

Tesla said in the 10-Q that in September it entered into three credit agreements with aggregate commitments of $30 billion; capital spending in the first nine months was $13.01 billion.
