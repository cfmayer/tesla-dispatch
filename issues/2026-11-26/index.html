The Morning Picture

Robotaxi's Dallas zone grows 83% as Tesla readies its chargers for the holiday

Tesla widened its driverless Robotaxi service area in Dallas on Wednesday, added cars and cut fares ahead of Thanksgiving, bringing its fully unsupervised zones across eight cities to 1,374 square miles. Tesla's website now lists FSD (Supervised) v15 as "rolling out," and Elon Musk said "Teslas have superhuman vision" over a clip of v15 driving through heavy rain.

Tesla Charging said it opened 1,027 Supercharger stalls at 91 US sites in the last 56 days and deployed 29 mobile stalls and attendants for the holiday rush, and two WattEV Megachargers went into service in Oakland. TSLA fell as much as 7% early on Wednesday before closing down 1.2% at $380.14. US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

"Just grew wider in Dallas"

The Dallas Robotaxi area nearly doubles to the north, with more cars and lower fares; Tesla's site now says v15 is rolling out.

"Just grew wider in Dallas. More cars, less $, Happy Thanksgiving!" Tesla's Robotaxi account posted at 1:28 p.m. EST Wednesday with a map of the expanded service area. The new boundary stretches north past University Park toward Farmers Branch, Addison and Richardson, TeslaNorth reported; Tesla gave no new fare or said how long the lower pricing will last, and did not say how many Cybercabs or Model Ys it added. The expansion comes less than a week after Cybercab rides started in Dallas, the second city after Austin to get the two-seater built without a steering wheel or pedals. TeslaNorth's latest check of Texas vehicle records found 822 Cybercabs and 734 Model Ys listed under Tesla's statewide Robotaxi authorization, and Tesla's Robotaxi safety counter passed six million estimated autonomous miles on Nov. 24.

Sawyer Merritt tallied the updated service areas at 1:29 p.m. EST: Austin 443 square miles, San Antonio 275, Houston 254, Dallas 150 ("expanded 83% today"), Fort Worth 143, Miami 59, Orlando 26 and Tampa 24, for a total of 1,374, all "fully autonomous/unsupervised."

v15 "rolling out," says Tesla's site

Tesla's Full Self-Driving page now shows FSD v15 rolling out, along with the v15 features such as Tesla Reality View and the FSD safety statistics, the Tesla owner account @BLKMDL3 posted at 4:37 p.m. EST with a screenshot. Tesla began sending v15 to Early Access Program members on Nov. 21 with "25x better handling of safety critical cases," Teslarati reported, and the release also drops the menu that asked drivers to give a reason every time they took over, Not a Tesla App noted. Musk has said v15 will reach more owners with next month's Holiday Update, the site added.

"Superhuman vision"

Musk quoted a clip at 3:21 a.m. EST Thursday with the words "Teslas have superhuman vision." The post, by an owner who goes by Rustavi, showed v15's on-screen visualization tracking the surrounding vehicles and the planned path while the windshield was flooded in heavy rain and the car displayed a "Camera Visibility Limited" warning.

Still Model Y only

v15 is still going only to Model Ys, the Model Y L included, the update tracker Teslascope said in replies on Wednesday (4:56 a.m. EST, 6:06 p.m. EST), adding that it will share news "the moment that changes." Older cars are still on v14.3 Lite: Teslascope posted at 12:01 a.m. EST Thursday that its 2018 Model 3 Performance with Hardware 3 drove a 100-mile streak on a trip from California to Northern Oregon through heavy rain, past a minor accident and with a smeary windshield, "no problem for Full Self-Driving V14.3 Lite."

Quick Hit

Bought for FSD. Baron Capital founder Ron Baron took delivery of a Model Y L in Cosmic Silver with a Zen Grey interior and said the Tesla office manager told him "everyone is buying the car for FSD," Sawyer Merritt posted at 3:15 p.m. EST.

Quick Hit

Fog and glare. "FSD v15 drove extremely well through about 100 miles of dense fog in Central CA yesterday," @BLKMDL3 wrote at 6:05 p.m. EST, after reporting that v15 "has no issues driving into direct rising sun so far."

II · Energy & Charging

1,027 new stalls before the holiday rush

Mobile Superchargers, site attendants and a waitlist pilot for Thanksgiving travel; two Megachargers open for WattEV's Semis in Oakland.

Tesla Charging listed its "preparations for Thanksgiving travel" at 2:35 p.m. EST Wednesday: 1,027 stalls across 91 sites opened in the last 56 days, "averaging ~18 new stalls a day in the U.S. alone"; higher uptime and service dispatch ahead of and during the travel window; 29 mobile stalls "strategically deployed for added travel capacity"; attendants for queue management at the busiest sites; and a waitlist pilot live at 300 sites. On Thursday morning it offered a Thanksgiving badge to drivers who charge at any US Supercharger today.

Megachargers for WattEV in Oakland

"2 WattEV Megachargers are now operational in Oakland, CA," Tesla Charging posted at 8:44 p.m. EST, promoting its Megacharger for Business program, under which Semi buyers host private charging sites. WattEV opened its Port of Oakland truck-charging depot on Monday and says more than 300 Semis will eventually serve routes from the port, TeslaNorth reported.

Five more Supercharger sites

Tesla Charging posted five openings on Wednesday: Kiruna, Sweden, 8 stalls (1:05 p.m. EST); East Indian School Road in Phoenix, 20 stalls (4:42 p.m.); Boulevard Lapinière in Brossard, Quebec, 16 stalls (7:39 p.m.); Cleveland Avenue in Santa Rosa, California, 16 stalls (9:58 p.m.); and Springdale, Utah, 8 stalls (10:49 p.m.).

Quick Hit

Off-grid oasis. Tesla Charging shared an owner's description of its Tesla Oasis site: 164 stalls, 12 for trailers, 11 MW of solar and 10 Megapacks storing up to 39 MWh, a site that "can run almost entirely off grid."

III · Markets & the Merger

A 7% dip, then most of it back

Tesla and SpaceX sank early on Wednesday amid pushback over a possible merger, then recovered by the close.

Tesla and SpaceX were "particularly hard-hit" early in Wednesday's broad sell-off "amid growing investor pushback over a potential merger and another delay in European regulatory approval for Tesla's Full Self-Driving software," Yahoo Finance reported, as AI-related stocks kept falling. The two companies have enlisted five directors and three investment banks to work on what would be the biggest merger in history, but have not announced a deal or terms, the report said. TSLA touched $357.64 in the 9:50 a.m. EST bar, down 7% from Tuesday's close, then rallied to finish at $380.14, down 1.2%; SpaceX ended up 1.3%. The European Union committee that could approve Tesla's supervised driving system bloc-wide now meets on Dec. 15, a European Commission spokesperson told Reuters, as published by CNBC.
