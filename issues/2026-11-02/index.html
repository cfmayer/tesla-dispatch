The Morning Picture

Robotaxi opens in Fort Worth

"Robotaxi available in Fort Worth starting tomorrow, 6am," Tesla's Robotaxi account posted at 12:36 a.m. EST Monday, which put the first rides at 7 a.m. EST (6 a.m. Central) on Monday, Nov 2. Fort Worth is Tesla's fourth Robotaxi city in Texas, after Austin, Dallas and Houston, and its first new city in about three months, Sawyer Merritt wrote five minutes later.

The launch map shows a single zone centered on downtown, reaching north toward Saginaw, west past White Settlement and southwest toward Benbrook, according to TeslaNorth, which noted that Tesla has not said how many vehicles will serve the city or whether rides will carry a safety monitor. Robotaxi hours run from 6 a.m. to 2 a.m. after last week's extension. Ashok Elluswamy, who leads Tesla's AI software, reposted the announcement at 1:20 a.m.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Tesla asks Australia for robotaxi trials in 2027

A Senate submission says FSD (Supervised) has driven 160 million kilometers in Australia and New Zealand, and test drives opened in Riga.

Tesla has asked the Australian government to speed up its autonomous vehicle rules and allow trials on public roads in 2027 instead of 2029, saying the change could save hundreds of lives and unlock more than $10 billion in economic and safety benefits, Sawyer Merritt posted at 10:42 a.m. EDT Friday. The submission went to a Senate committee inquiry into productivity in Australia.

Tesla says its cars have driven over 160 million kilometers (about 100 million miles) on FSD (Supervised) in Australia and New Zealand since it launched there in September 2025, and that drivers using it saw 40% fewer collisions, 90% fewer harsh braking incidents and 86% fewer harsh acceleration incidents. "Australian road fatalities sadly hit a 15-year high in 2025," the company wrote (Merritt).

Test drives open in Riga

Tesla began public FSD (Supervised) test drives in Riga, Latvia, on Saturday, a week after Latvia approved the feature, TeslaNorth reported. The site put the subscription price in Latvia at €99 a month.

Quick Hit

Turo pitches FSD. The car-rental marketplace Turo sent customers an email promoting Tesla's FSD (Supervised): "take a Tesla with FSD (Supervised) on your weekend getaway," Merritt posted at 2:41 p.m. EDT Friday.

An F1 champion hands over the wheel

"Even the world's best drivers like being driven," Tesla posted at 12:07 p.m. EDT Friday about Nico Rosberg, the 2016 Formula One world champion, who has been testing a Cybertruck with FSD (Supervised) around San Francisco. "My whole racing career, I was the one in control behind the wheel. But I'm now fully willing to hand over control," Rosberg said, as quoted in the post. "Even F1 champions love Tesla self-driving," Musk wrote at 5:55 p.m.

Quick Hit

"Bone voyage." For Halloween, a Cybercab drove itself around Austin with skeletons inside, the Robotaxi account posted early Saturday (Merritt).

II · Sales & Demand

Cybercab starts a 12-city tour of Europe

The two-seat robotaxi goes on display in Zurich on Thursday and ends the tour in Vienna in mid-January.

"Cybercab is back," Tesla Europe posted at 3:24 a.m. EST Monday. "Starting November 5, experience how we are building the future of autonomy." Tesla's event page lists 12 stops, starting in Zurich from Nov. 5 to 8 and ending in Vienna from Jan. 14 to 17. The car will be shown on display; Tesla has not offered rides.

The long Model Y

"Tesla Model Y long wheel base variant," Musk posted at 11:28 a.m. EDT Friday, quoting an owner's video about the six-seat Model Y L, which, the owner wrote, has "all the little things" that "make it an amazing vehicle."

Rivian jumps as tech sells off

Rivian shares rose 6.20% on Friday and General Motors gained 1.96%, while Ford slipped 0.89%, according to Yahoo Finance data. Over five sessions GM is up 15.12%, against Tesla's 7.41%.

Quick Hit

Grok on older cars? Software update 2026.38 added the files for Grok to Model 3 and Model Y cars with Intel computers, and one owner with root access turned it on, Not a Tesla App reported Saturday. Tesla has not said whether it will enable it.

III · Energy & Charging

Owners pick 36 more Supercharger sites

The third-quarter vote winners span 19 markets, and a new round of voting is open.

Tesla Charging named 36 community-voted Supercharger sites for the third quarter on Wednesday, across 19 markets from Hanksville, Utah, to Miyazu, Japan, and opened voting for the fourth quarter, Not a Tesla App reported Friday. Owners can cast up to five votes per cycle on Tesla's voting portal, and the next winners will be announced early next year. The network recently passed 85,000 stalls at 9,000 locations, the site said.

The cost of Emergency Drive Away

A Tesla's Emergency Drive Away feature lets an owner break the car free of a Supercharger stall, but one owner found it requires a service visit even if the car never moves, Not a Tesla App reported at 4:00 p.m. EDT Saturday.

IV · New Supercharger & Megacharger Sites

Twenty-five sites, 225 stalls

Three 16-stall stations in Simi Valley, Albany and Wikieup led the week; nine sites charge at up to 500 kW. No new Megacharger site was posted.

Tesla Charging, the company's charging account on X, announced 25 new Supercharger sites with 225 stalls from Monday, Oct 26, through Sunday, Nov 1: 15 in the United States with 153 stalls, two in Canada with 16, four in Europe with 36 and four in Asia and the Pacific with 20. All 25 are listed as open, and every stall is a V4 post, according to the volunteer-run tracker supercharge.info.

The largest are three 16-stall stations: at Harley's Valley Bowl on Cochran Street in Simi Valley, California, posted at 4:05 p.m. EDT Tuesday; at a WinCo Foods on Pacific Boulevard Southeast in Albany, Oregon (post); and at a Chevron on U.S. Route 93 in Wikieup, Arizona (post). Simi Valley and Albany are listed at 500 kW and Wikieup at 325 kW (supercharge.info).

The 500 kW sites

Nine sites are listed at 500 kW: seven in the United States, in Bedminster, New Jersey; Tillamook and Albany, Oregon; Reynoldsburg, Ohio; Walla Walla, Washington; Simi Valley; and Manchester, Tennessee, plus Hannover, Germany, at a Netto store, and Érd, Hungary, at a Tesco. Tillamook's eight stalls at a Fred Meyer include four trailer-friendly ones, and Reynoldsburg's site at a Target is open 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. (supercharge.info). Tesla called Walla Walla a "Voting Winner Site" (post), and on Wednesday it named a new list of winners and opened another voting round (post).

The capital region

Four sites with 36 stalls opened around Washington this week: eight stalls at a Lowe's on Market Street Northeast in Washington, D.C. (post); eight at a Royal Farms in Bealeton, Virginia, open around the clock; eight at a Royal Farms on Fort Meade Road in Laurel, Maryland; and 12 at Lorton Marketplace in Lorton, Virginia, posted at 2:38 p.m. EST Sunday (post). All four are listed at 325 kW.

Open to other EVs

Two sites list both NACS and CCS1 connectors at every stall, so other EVs can plug in without an adapter: Laurel, Maryland, and East Wenatchee, Washington. The sites in Hannover; Érd and Zalaegerszeg, Hungary; Bollnäs, Sweden; Auckland, New Zealand; and Hong Kong use CCS2 plugs. Bangkok, Thailand, and Tokyo's Edogawa ward are listed for Teslas only.

One U.S. site belongs to Supercharger for Business, Tesla's program for stations owned by other businesses: EVODS's six stalls on Valley Mall Parkway in East Wenatchee, Washington (post). The program "grew to over 600 stalls," Tesla said in its third-quarter shareholder update.

Megachargers

Tesla Charging posted no new Megacharger site this week. The shareholder update says Tesla "deployed the first pre-assembled Megacharger to support the Tesla Semi ramp" (Exhibit 99.1).

V · Optimus & AI

"No" to a December Optimus waitlist

Musk rejected a report that Tesla would open Optimus reservations in early December.

The San Francisco Standard reported on Friday that Tesla would open reservations for its Optimus humanoid robot around the Humanoids Summit on Dec. 1 and 2, citing the summit's founder, Modar Alaoui. Musk replied "No" at 6:08 p.m. EDT, which Merritt shared as Musk's answer to the report.

"Transformers can do anything"

Elluswamy posted the line at 1:03 p.m. EDT Friday, referring to the neural-network design behind modern AI models; the Optimus account replied with a flexed robot arm.

Quick Hit

"Meaningful wealth." "Tesla and SpaceX have created meaningful wealth for hundreds of thousands of employees over time and improved the retirement savings of hundreds of millions of people," Musk posted at 11:24 a.m. EDT Saturday.
