The Morning Picture

Europe pushes Tesla's software meeting to Dec. 15 as v15 reaches older Model Ys

The European Union committee that could approve Tesla's supervised driver-assistance system across the bloc will now meet on Dec. 15 instead of Dec. 1, a European Commission spokesperson told Reuters on Wednesday. At home, FSD (Supervised) v15.0.1 has begun reaching Model Y cars of the previous design that have Tesla's AI4 computer but no front bumper camera, a sign the rollout is widening beyond the refreshed crossover.

Tesla raised the Cyberbeast's price by another $5,000 to $114,990 and stopped offering Cybertruck leases, General Motors said it will adopt Tesla's charging connector in South Korea, and WattEV put its first Tesla Semis to work at the Port of Oakland. TSLA slipped 0.2% to $384.68 on Tuesday, and Seaport Global started coverage at Buy with a $475 target.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Europe's decision moves to Dec. 15

The EU vehicle committee has twice discussed the Dutch approval without voting; v15 spreads to more Model Ys in the US.

A European Union meeting expected to consider Tesla's bid to win approval for its assisted self-driving system across the bloc "has been postponed for Dec. 15," a European Commission spokesperson told Reuters on Wednesday. The Technical Committee for Motor Vehicles (TCMV) had been due to meet on Dec. 1, according to the Dutch regulator RDW; Belgium's Flanders transport ministry first said the meeting had moved to Dec. 7 before the Commission settled on Dec. 15. The committee, where member states rule on vehicle approvals, held no vote at two earlier meetings after the Netherlands granted preliminary approval in April. In Europe the system is called Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD), a name adopted after regulators raised concerns about "Full Self-Driving." EU-wide approval needs at least 15 of the 27 member countries representing 65% of the bloc's population, and nearly a dozen countries have followed the Dutch lead, Italy the most populous; a rejection at EU level would invalidate those national approvals. The countries that already allow the system hold about 28.7% of the EU's population, and a vote on Dec. 15 is possible but not required, Numerama noted. No reason for the date changes has been made public.

v15 on a Model Y without a bumper camera

"Legacy HW4 Model Y getting v15 now!!!" an owner who goes by Noah (@noahTLW) posted at 1:45 p.m. EST Tuesday with app screenshots showing build 2026.38.6.11 with FSD (Supervised) v15.0.1 downloading. It is the same build Tesla began rolling out on Nov. 21, TeslaNorth reported, and early installs had mostly been on refreshed Model Ys; the download shows the bumper camera is not required for every car, though it says nothing about how widely older Model Ys are being offered the update, or about Hardware 3. Sawyer Merritt wrote at 2:06 p.m. EST that v15 "is now starting to roll out to Model Ys with no front bumper camera." Musk has said the wide release is planned for December.

"FSD Supervised can change your life"

Tesla quoted owner Justin Strafuss (@MstrHappyJustin) at 3:09 p.m. EST Tuesday with that line. In his thread Strafuss wrote: "I've lived with cerebral palsy my whole life. Over my last 9,000 miles in my Tesla, 99.4% were on FSD (Supervised)."

Musk on what the driving model does

Replying to @davepl1968, Musk wrote at 11:34 a.m. EST Tuesday: "Another way to say it is semantic compression together with correlating an input bitstream to an output bitstream, for example: given this bitstream of video & vehicle data, output these driving commands."

II · Sales & Demand

Cyberbeast up another $5,000; leases end

The only Cybertruck trim still on sale in the US now costs $114,990, and buyers must pay cash or finance.

Tesla raised the US price of the Cyberbeast to $114,990 from $109,990 and stopped offering leases on new Cybertruck orders, Not a Tesla App reported. New orders show estimated deliveries between March and June 2027, near the end of summer 2027, when Tesla plans to stop building the truck. The price matches what the Cyberbeast cost early this year, but without the Luxe Package extras, such as FSD for life and free Supercharging, that it included until February. The trim cost $102,490 on Nov. 6 and $109,990 on Nov. 10, after Tesla paused Dual Motor and then Premium AWD orders, the site's price table shows. "Tesla has also ended leases for all new orders. You can only pay cash for the truck or finance," Sawyer Merritt posted at 10:39 p.m. EST Tuesday.

III · Energy & Charging

GM brings Tesla's plug to South Korea

GM Korea will sell electric vehicles with Tesla's connector; six new Supercharger sites opened on two continents.

General Motors said Wednesday it will adopt Tesla's North American Charging Standard (NACS) for its electric vehicles in South Korea under an agreement with Tesla, Yonhap reported. GM Korea plans to introduce models with NACS ports that can use Tesla's Supercharger network; GM's EVs in the country have so far used Combined Charging System 1 (CCS1) ports, the domestic fast-charging standard. The deal extends to South Korea the NACS partnership GM and Tesla set up in 2023; existing Korean-market GM EVs with CCS1 ports can use NACS chargers with an adapter, JKN reported. GM has said all its 2027 model-year Chevrolet, Cadillac and GMC EVs will come with NACS ports as standard.

Six new Supercharger sites

Tesla Charging posted six openings on Tuesday: Mount Crawford, Virginia, with 16 stalls (1:46 p.m. EST); Biervliet in the Netherlands, 8 stalls at Groen Goud, "the first Supercharger for Business site in the Netherlands" (3:33 p.m.); Hummelstown, Pennsylvania, 8 stalls, a site picked in Tesla's public vote (4:40 p.m.); West Frye Road in Chandler, Arizona, 8 stalls (7:00 p.m.); Commonwealth Avenue in Jacksonville, Florida, 12 stalls (8:35 p.m.); and Veradale, Washington, 8 stalls (10:38 p.m.).

Quick Hit

Powershare. "Testing out Powershare Home Backup in Texas," Tesla Energy posted at 12:42 p.m. EST, sharing a video from Good Faith Energy, which simulated an outage with the app's "Go Off-Grid" button: after sunset, a Cybertruck powered the house while recharging its Powerwall 3 system.

IV · Semi, Chips & the Merger

WattEV's first Semis haul from Oakland

A new truck-charging depot at the port, a memory squeeze that already trimmed AI4.5, and a quiet day for Tesla stock amid merger talk.

WattEV opened a public charging depot for electric trucks at the Port of Oakland on Monday and introduced the first Tesla Semis that will haul freight from the port across the Bay Area and Northern California, TeslaNorth reported. The depot, WattEV's eighth heavy-duty site in California and open to other fleets, can charge up to 25 trucks at once at up to 240 kilowatts (kW) each, or seven at 1.2 megawatts (MW). WattEV says more than 300 Semis will eventually serve a corridor to Sacramento, the Central Valley and Nevada; it did not say how many trucks it introduced. In May it announced a deal for 370 Semis in California, with deliveries of the first 50 starting in 2026.

The memory squeeze behind AI4.5

Dell chief operating officer Jeff Clarke said on Tuesday's earnings call that the shortage of memory chips "continues clearly into 2027" as chipmakers shift factories to high-bandwidth memory for AI processors, and HP's finance chief said memory and storage had risen to nearly 40 cents of every dollar it spends on PC parts, from 15 to 18 cents at the start of its fiscal year, Stocktwits reported. The same squeeze has reached Tesla: "RAM is super expensive, so we reduced AI4.5 from 32GB to 24GB," Musk said on Sunday, according to the report, adding that "it's hard to use 32GB anyway, given memory bandwidth constraints."

Tesla holds steady as SpaceX slides

Tesla closed down 0.2% on Tuesday while SpaceX fell 5.65%. In The Motley Fool, Lee Samaha described the gap as "classic pre-deal positioning" on the day the Financial Times reported that five directors, two from SpaceX and three from Tesla, had been appointed to committees discussing a possible merger. In an all-stock deal, he wrote, investors buy the company expected to be acquired (Tesla) and sell the acquirer (SpaceX).
