The Morning Picture

Finland clears Tesla Assisted Driving as Tesla edges past Waymo in Texas

Finland's transport agency recognized the Dutch approval of Tesla Assisted Driving, the European name for FSD (Supervised), on Tuesday, making it the 11th European country to allow the system, Traficom said on Wednesday. "Rollout begins soon," Tesla Europe posted.

In Texas, the state's automated-vehicle register now lists 1,302 vehicles for Tesla's Robotaxi service against 1,297 for Waymo, the first time Tesla has led, EVwire reported. Tesla also opened a six-stall Semi Megacharger near Chicago, and a Tesla tuner offered to take over the Cybertruck line. TSLA rose 0.5% on Tuesday to $379.78.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

Finland becomes the 11th European approval

Traficom moves ahead of an EU-wide vote so owners can learn the system before the hardest winter weather; Tesla's registered Texas Robotaxi fleet passes Waymo's.

The Finnish Transport and Communications Agency (Traficom) recognized the provisional type-approval granted by the Dutch authority on Nov 10, so Tesla can start offering Tesla Assisted Driving to Finnish customers immediately, the agency said. Finland is moving "slightly faster" than the EU process; a final EU-wide decision needs a vote in the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV), expected during the coming winter, and takes effect about three weeks after a successful vote. Traficom said national recognition does not bind Finland's vote.

The agency tied the decision to changes Tesla has made. References to self-driving were removed from the name, including in Finnish- and Swedish-language materials. Drivers can no longer have the system hold a speed up to 50% above the limit, and speeding settings reset to the limit whenever the car restarts. "The system's design and approval assessment have taken winter conditions into account, but its operation may be subject to various limitations if the cameras become covered in snow, slush or other dirt, or if traffic conditions become more challenging than usual," said Senior Specialist Jukka Juhola. Ten countries were already on the Dutch authority's Nov 3 list, Tesla Mag noted.

Tesla passes Waymo on the Texas register

A batch of 20 Model Ys just before midnight on Nov 10, Texas time, took Tesla Robotaxi from 1,282 to 1,302 registered vehicles, five more than Waymo's 1,297, according to EVwire, which tracks the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles register. Tesla's list holds 729 Model Ys and 573 Cybercabs; Cybercab registrations have held at 573 since Oct 27 while Tesla added 222 Model Ys. On May 28 the counts were 42 for Tesla and 619 for Waymo.

The register counts vehicles authorized for driverless operation, not cars carrying riders, and gives no service area or trip counts, TeslaNorth cautioned; on Nov 1 the counts were 1,204 for Tesla and 1,259 for Waymo. "Tesla has just officially surpassed Waymo in the total number of robotaxis registered to date in Texas for the first time," Sawyer Merritt (@SawyerMerritt) posted at 6:17 p.m. EST Tuesday.

v14.2 Lite reaches HW3 cars in Korea

Software update 2026.38.4.5, first seen on Nov 10, carries FSD (Supervised) v14.2 Lite for Hardware 3 (HW3) cars, TeslaMagz reported from the release notes, which say Tesla "distilled the intelligence from HW4 V14 into HW3" and add unparking and reversing, and Speed Profiles. In South Korea, owner Tsla Chan (@Tslachan) received it that day, posted a 56-minute drive in a 2022 Model 3 and reported quicker decisions, but still pressed the accelerator at a tollgate and an unprotected left turn, and said parking remained poor, TeslaNorth reported. US HW3 cars already have v14.3 Lite.

Quick Hit

Cybercab in Korea. Tesla Korea will show the Cybercab at Starfield Anseong from Nov 12 to 15, its first display in the country; the car is not for sale there and no Korean Robotaxi service date has been announced, Car-ticle reported.

Tesla confronts a Chinese outlet over unlocked FSD

Tesla's legal department sent a letter to D1EV, a Chinese electric-vehicle media site, after an "Intelligent Driving Competition" it livestreamed from Changsha on Nov 8 used a Model Y running FSD v14.2.2 that had been activated through unofficial channels; FSD v14 is not approved for release in China. Tesla demanded that D1EV delete the videos, supply the vehicle identification numbers (VINs) within 48 hours so the software can be restored, and stop reverse-engineering Tesla systems, or it would report the matter to police, CarNewsChina reported. Chief executive Pang Yicheng refused (translated): "If your legal department believes it is necessary to take action, then please do so. We will not remove the content."

II · Sales & Demand

A tuner offers to keep the Cybertruck alive

Unplugged Performance proposes an investor-funded Cybertruck line that pays Tesla a license fee; Tesla says many deposits will be refunded before the Texas line closes.

Unplugged Performance, the Hawthorne, California, Tesla aftermarket specialist, publicly offered on Tuesday to take the Cybertruck line off Tesla's hands, funded by a consortium of investors and run at no cost to Tesla's floor space, headcount or focus, Teslarati reported. Tesla would collect a license fee plus FSD and Robotaxi network revenue on every vehicle built, and Cybercab would get first call on battery cells. The company would build the Cybertruck, a three-row CyberSUV and an unmanned CyberCargo hauler.

The post passed 240,000 views within hours, and neither Musk nor Tesla had responded, Electrek reported. A follow-up invited potential investors to get in touch and said it was only gauging interest, not offering securities, Teslarati added.

Refunds ahead for many orders

Musk has said Tesla will "try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people," because "the Cybertruck order rate far exceeds our production rate," Not a Tesla App reported. Tesla has not said how it will choose which orders to fill; the site expects the paused Dual Motor AWD, launched at $59,990 in February, to be hit hardest.

III · Energy & Charging

A Semi Megacharger for Chicago's freight belt

Six truck stalls open in Hodgkins, Illinois, as Superchargers go live from South Padre Island to Latvia.

Tesla added a six-stall Megacharger for the Semi in Hodgkins, Illinois, southwest of Chicago, the Tesla Charging account posted at 9:21 p.m. EST Tuesday. Megachargers deliver up to 1.2 megawatts and can restore up to 60% of a Semi's range in 30 minutes, depending on conditions, and serve only Tesla Semi trucks, TeslaNorth reported. The site follows an eight-stall Megacharger in Conley, Georgia, and a four-stall site in Tulare, California, last month.

New Superchargers on both sides of the Atlantic

Since Tuesday morning the account has also announced Superchargers at South Padre Island, Texas (16 stalls on Padre Boulevard), Carbondale, Colorado (8), West Memphis, Arkansas (8), Sunne, Sweden (8), Morschwiller, France (6) and Liepāja, Latvia (8).

IV · Rules & the Courts

Tesla asks for camera-era headlight rules

A letter to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration backs rewriting the lighting standard but wants more carmakers at the table; the SEC winds up the 2018 "funding secured" fund.

In a Nov 9 letter to National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) Administrator Jonathan Morrison, Tesla backed modernizing Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) No. 108, the US lighting rule, but said testing must account for matrix LED headlights steered by cameras and software, TeslaNorth reported. "Tesla designs and integrates exterior lighting as part of a software-defined vehicle," wrote Suraj Nagaraj, senior director of safety and homologation engineering. "Glare, upper- and lower-beam photometry, aim and mounting height, and adaptive-beam performance cannot be resolved by lamp-level laboratory points alone," the letter states. Tesla objected that only two of the proposed advisory committee's 18 seats go to individual automakers, against four for lighting makers or retailers and four for manufacturing associations.

SEC closes the "funding secured" fund

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) asked a Manhattan federal judge on Tuesday to close the Fair Fund created from the $20 million civil fines each paid by Musk and Tesla to settle the 2018 case over his "funding secured" posts, Reuters reported. A claims administrator paid $41.53 million, including interest, to 3,350 eligible claimants, who received 51.7% of their approved claims; 3,046 claims were rejected, and about $449,000 left over goes to the US Treasury.
