The Morning Picture

Roadster night: the livestream starts at 8:55 p.m. EDT

Tesla shows its new Roadster tonight near Waco, Texas, nearly nine years after the first reveal. "The new Tesla Roadster is a candidate for greatest product demo of all time," Elon Musk posted late Wednesday, and later added that the demo "will be a BANGER, probably metaphorically, though some chance literally." Tesla's own account asked, "Anything happening tomorrow?"

FSD (Supervised) v14.3 Lite is reaching older Hardware 3 Model S and Model X cars for the first time. TSLA fell 2.15% to $367.16 as a hotter-than-expected inflation report sent the Nasdaq 100 down 2.02%. Tesla has filed nothing new with the SEC.

Tonight · Roadster livestream

The new Roadster goes live tonight

Thu Oct 15 · stream starts 8:55 p.m. EDT (7:55 p.m. Central) · on Tesla's X account

Tesla set the time on X. The invite-only, outdoor event is for reservation holders who received tickets, Business Insider reported; Tesla is taking $50,000 deposits, and production timing is still unclear, Axios reported. What to watch: whether SpaceX cold-gas thrusters appear on the car, final specifications and price, and a production date. In 2017 Musk promised 0–60 mph in 1.9 seconds and a 250 mph top speed.

I · Full Self-Driving (FSD)

v14 Lite reaches the oldest Model S and Model X

Intel-based HW3 cars get the distilled branch, with some features held back.

FSD (Supervised) v14.3 Lite

FSD v14 Lite is rolling out to Intel-powered HW3 Model S and Model X cars as v14.3 Lite in software 2026.38.5.1, the first time these older vehicles have received the distilled branch, Not a Tesla App reported. The release notes say Tesla "Distilled the intelligence from HW4 V14 into HW3." On these cars the build includes Speed Profiles and an FSD streak counter that tracks distance driven without an intervention, but leaves out Arrival Options, starting FSD from Park and reversing on FSD, which HW3 Model 3 and Model Y cars with Intel processors get.

School-zone petition

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is still weighing whether a petition over robotaxis allegedly speeding through school zones becomes a formal investigation; it has until about January 21, 2027, Electrek reported. Tesla's answers to NHTSA's Special Order on the Cybercab are due October 30.

II · Sales & Products

Seven reveal dates later

The Roadster was first shown in November 2017 with production promised for 2020.

Tesla has not delivered a single new Roadster; the reveal moved from late 2025 to April 1, 2026, then late April, late May or June, August, October 1 and finally tonight, which Electrek counts as at least seven unveil dates. The October 1 event was called off because of the weather; the show "can only be held outdoors," Tesla said. Musk has claimed 0–60 mph in under one second with SpaceX cold-gas thrusters, and the demo car is reportedly remote-operated and not street-legal, Electrek reported. "I think it might be one of the most spectacular demos ever," Musk said on an April earnings call, according to Axios.

Quick Hit

"Major threshold." Quoting a post saying gasoline-only cars had fallen below 50% of global new-vehicle sales for the first time, Musk wrote: "Almost all cars will be electric in the future."

III · Energy & Charging

How long a Model Y can run a house

Tesla's Powershare page puts numbers on Tuesday's Home Backup expansion.

With a Powerwall, Model 3 and Model Y cars can extend a home's backup power "by over two days," and a Cybertruck by over three days, based on average US use of 30 kWh a day, Tesla says. All Model 3 and Model Y cars ordered in the US and Puerto Rico after October 1, 2026, are capable of Powershare; some built earlier are too. Owners can check under Software, Additional Vehicle Info, for "Powershare Support: Enabled." The function runs through the cars' PCS2 Lite bidirectional inverters, certified to the UL 1741 standard.
