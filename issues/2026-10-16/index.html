The Morning Picture

The Roadster flies, and it is two years away

Tesla's new Roadster drove up a towering curved ramp, hung upside down on fan suction, flipped in the air on SpaceX cold-gas thrusters and later hopped over a Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y and Model 3 at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas, on Thursday night, Reuters reported. A timed run reached 60 mph in a claimed 0.81 seconds. Musk said production starts in 2028. "Turns out the future does have flying cars," he posted.

No price was given. Tesla is taking $50,000 deposits for the Performance version and $250,000 for Thrust; Rocket is invite-only. TSLA slipped 1.29% to $362.44 before the show and was up 5% in premarket trading Friday, according to Reuters. Third-quarter results come Wednesday.

Wed Oct 21 · results after the close · webcast 5:30 p.m. EDT

Tesla set the date in an 8-K on October 2, when it reported 486,532 deliveries and 13.7 GWh of storage deployed. Analysts expect earnings of $0.45 a share on $28.11 billion in revenue, according to Yahoo Finance. "The margin and cash flow numbers will carry far more weight with investors than anything they saw at the reveal," eToro analyst Josh Gilbert told Reuters.

I · The Roadster

Suction, thrusters and three trims

Tesla showed the car nearly nine years after the first reveal and named 2028 for production.

What it did

A fan system under the car provides up to 2 g of suction, Tesla says, enough to hold it on an inverted, Hot Wheels-like ramp; at the top, SpaceX-supplied thrusters rated at more than 80 kilonewtons flew it forward through a barrel roll and eased it down, Car and Driver reported. The first acceleration run was timed at about one second; after Musk was reminded he had promised less, a second run came in at a claimed 0.81 seconds. Tesla said that is "0.67 seconds (w/ rollout subtracted)." To a post noting 0.81 seconds is "over 3.3 Gs," Musk replied, "You're right, we can do better."

How it works

Engineering vice president Lars Moravy said the car uses 10 thrusters fed by 600 liters of rechargeable compressed air, TechCrunch reported; The Verge put the load at about 250 kilograms of air. "Every usable void in the car is packed with Carbon Fiber Overwrapped Pressure Vessels (COPVs)," Tesla posted, adding that the thrusters generate "4–5x the force the tires can."

Trims and numbers

  • Performance ("road ready," two seats): active aerodynamics and suction; 0–60 mph under 1.6 seconds and more than 300 miles of range, per The Verge. $50,000 deposit.
  • Thrust ("race ready," two seats, limited release): rear cold-gas thrusters for 0–60 mph in under 1 second, per Tesla. $250,000 deposit.
  • Rocket ("air ready," one seat, invite only): thrusters around the car; "So yes, it can fly." Tesla says it is "not approved for public road use."

Moravy put top speed above 230 mph; Musk said "I think you've got to crack halfway to the speed of sound," Reuters reported. The range is less than half of the 620 miles promised in 2017. Deepwater's Gene Munster called it "an engineering flex with no commercial value beyond embarrassing traditional car makers."

II · Full Self-Driving (FSD) & Robotaxi

Remote operators behind five robotaxi crashes

Two new federal crash reports involve a human driving remotely.

Tesla's latest Standing General Order filings with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) add two August crashes, in Dallas and Austin, in which a remote support operator was driving a Model Y when it touched an SUV at about 0.4 mph and a dumpster at about 1 mph, Electrek reported. No passengers were aboard. That makes five remote takeovers in Tesla's crash data, all ending in contact, out of 36 robotaxi incident reports. Tesla does not disclose how often remote operators intervene.

Quick Hit

"The FSD version of this will be something." Google's Sundar Pichai congratulated Musk after the reveal; Musk replied, "It's such an honor working with incredible engineers." Separately, he wrote that "Cybercab will be a gamechanger."
